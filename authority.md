# Authority

Authority is a social agreement that grants someone or something the power to influence outcomes. Sustainable authority depends on clarity, reciprocity, and accountability rather than mere position.

## Foundations
- **Legibility:** People need to understand who holds authority, what decisions they can make, and why those decisions are legitimate.
- **Reciprocity:** Authority is steadier when it responds to the people it affects—listening, adapting, and explaining changes.
- **Competence:** Expertise and consistent follow-through keep authority credible beyond titles or formal roles.

## Failure modes
- **Opacity:** Decisions without visible reasoning erode trust and invite speculation.
- **Overreach:** Claiming power without mandate or capacity creates resentment and brittle systems.
- **Neglect:** Ignoring feedback or consequences turns authority into inertia, leaving gaps others will fill informally.

## Practicing responsible authority
- Make decision pathways transparent and document the principles behind them.
- Share power where possible through delegation, consultation, and clear feedback loops.
- Periodically audit whether authority still aligns with community needs and whether successors are prepared.

When authority is treated as a maintained relationship rather than a permanent possession, it can protect shared goals while staying adaptable to changing realities.
