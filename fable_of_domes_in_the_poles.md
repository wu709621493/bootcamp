# Fable of Domes in the Poles

Long ago, when the cold still felt like a question and the maps were shy about their edges, two domes rose at the ends of the world. The northern dome was spun from aurora glass, glowing with every story the wind carried. The southern dome was woven from snow-quartz and silence, holding the wisdom of what had never been said.

The travelers who reached these domes believed they were shelters against the endless night. Yet the domes were not walls; they were listening bowls. They gathered voices, clouds, and footfalls, turning them into echoes that could be heard across oceans.

## The travelers and their lessons
- **The cartographer** arrived with ink frozen in his pen. The northern dome melted it, and he learned that maps must include uncertainty, or they are only instructions for getting lost.
- **The sailor** came with a compass that spun in circles. The southern dome stilled it, teaching her that some directions are found by standing still and letting the world move around you.
- **The poet** brought a lantern that burned too hot. Both domes cooled the flame to a steady glow, reminding him that warmth is truer when shared than when it dazzles.

## The pledge of the domes
Each year, when the light returns, the domes release their gathered echoes into the sky. The north sends stories of change; the south sends stories of stillness. Together they keep the poles from becoming lonely, and they remind the rest of the world that endurance is not the same as silence.

Those who hear the echoes say the domes are still there, shimmering like questions you can only answer by traveling. And so the fable ends as it began: with a choice to walk toward the edge of the map, and to listen when you arrive.
