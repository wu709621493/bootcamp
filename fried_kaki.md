# Fried Kaki (Crispy Japanese-Style Fried Oysters)

Golden, crunchy oysters inspired by Japanese kaki fry. This version uses a quick brine for plumpness and panko for an airy crust.

## Ingredients (Serves 2)
- 12 shucked oysters, drained
- 1 cup cold water + 1 tsp salt (for brining)
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1 cup panko breadcrumbs
- Neutral oil for frying (enough for 1 1/2–2 inches in a pot)
- Lemon wedges, for serving
- Tonkatsu sauce or tartar sauce, for serving
- Pinch of salt and pepper

## Equipment
- Small bowl for brine
- Three shallow bowls for breading
- Paper towels
- Dutch oven or deep, heavy pot
- Spider or slotted spoon
- Instant-read thermometer (recommended)

## Preparation
1. **Quick brine:** Stir salt into cold water. Add oysters and soak 10 minutes to season and plump. Drain well and pat completely dry.
2. **Set up breading:** Place flour, beaten egg, and panko in separate shallow bowls. Season panko with a pinch of salt and pepper.

## Cooking
1. **Heat oil:** Add oil to the pot to a depth of about 1 1/2–2 inches. Heat to 350°F/175°C.
2. **Bread oysters:** Dredge each oyster in flour (shake off excess), dip in egg, then press into panko until fully coated.
3. **Fry in batches:** Fry 4–6 oysters at a time for 2–3 minutes until deep golden brown. Maintain oil between 325–350°F/165–175°C; avoid crowding.
4. **Drain:** Transfer to a paper towel-lined rack. Sprinkle lightly with salt while hot.

## Serving
- Serve immediately with lemon wedges and tonkatsu or tartar sauce.
- Pair with shredded cabbage or a simple green salad to balance the richness.

## Tips for Success
- **Dry thoroughly:** Excess moisture can pop in hot oil and softens the crust—pat oysters dry after brining.
- **Oil temperature control:** If the oil drops too low, the coating absorbs more oil; too hot and the panko scorches. Work in small batches and give the oil a moment to recover between batches.
- **Pre-warm panko (optional):** Lightly toast panko in a dry pan before breading for an extra-nutty crunch.
