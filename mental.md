# Mental

Mental is the quiet interior weather of a person—the hush before a decision, the surge after a loss, the steady light that keeps attention from drifting. It is not a single mood but a living spectrum of focus, memory, doubt, and curiosity, each layer shifting with time, sleep, and trust. A mental state can be foggy or luminous, brittle or elastic, and all are transient, not verdicts.

Mental strength is often imagined as a wall, yet it behaves more like a riverbank. It holds shape while still letting the current pass. It learns, softens, and re-forms after storms. The most resilient mental habit is not denial of pain, but the ability to name it and still move with dignity.

A healthy mental life is built on small agreements with the self:

- Keep a rhythm of rest, so thoughts have room to settle.
- Practice attention, so the present is not swallowed by the past or future.
- Share burdens early, so silence does not become a prison.
- Treat mistakes as information, not identity.

Mental care is communal as much as personal. Language, safety, and belonging are scaffolds for the mind. When we grant people time to recover, space to think, and permission to ask for help, we enlarge the mental room in which everyone can breathe.
