# Steam Rack

A steam rack is the quiet scaffolding of a kitchen: a perforated platform that lifts food above simmering water so heat arrives as gentle vapor instead of aggressive boil. It is a tool for patience. The steam does not sear; it coaxes, carrying moisture and aroma upward in soft waves, wrapping vegetables, fish, and dumplings in a humid envelope that protects their structure while surrendering their starches.

When you use a steam rack, you are designing distance. The gap between the water and the food is where texture is negotiated. Too close and droplets leap, saturating what should stay tender; too far and the steam thins, struggling to finish its work. A good rack keeps that balance, letting heat and moisture rise evenly so the food cooks in a calm, consistent rhythm.

In practice, the rack is also a promise of cleanliness. It prevents direct contact with the pot, which means flavors stay true and delicate. The dumpling skins remain translucent rather than soggy, the fish flakes without falling apart, and the greens stay bright instead of drab. It is a small, stainless bridge between boiling water and finished plate, and it teaches the cook to respect the slow, steady physics of steam.
