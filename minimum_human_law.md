# Minimum Human Law: A Compact Framework for Entire Humanity

## Why a minimal code is necessary
- **Interdependence at scale**: Global supply chains, climate systems, and digital networks knit humanity together; fragmented rules generate friction and risk.
- **Legitimacy through clarity**: A short, principled code is easier to teach, translate, and monitor, reducing selective enforcement and power abuse.
- **Adaptive foundation**: Minimal rules create space for local norms and innovation while preserving a shared baseline of dignity and safety.

## Core obligations (five articles)
1. **Non-harm**: Do not intentionally inflict serious physical, psychological, or ecological harm on others; prevent such harm when reasonably able.
2. **Reciprocal fairness**: Honor agreements made freely; obtain informed consent for actions that affect others' bodies, data, or property.
3. **Integrity of knowledge**: Do not fabricate or suppress material facts in public decision-making; maintain transparent records for collective risks.
4. **Stewardship of commons**: Use shared resources—air, water, biodiversity, orbital space—without degrading their viability for others or future generations.
5. **Rescue and refuge**: Render aid to persons in imminent peril when it does not impose comparable risk to oneself; protect the vulnerable displaced by disaster or conflict.

## Implementation principles
- **Proportional enforcement**: Sanctions scale with intent, impact, and remediation effort; restorative options preferred where victims agree.
- **Due process**: Neutral adjudicators, the right to be heard, and access to evidence are mandatory in every forum, from local councils to international tribunals.
- **Open standards**: Publish procedural rules, data formats, and auditing methods so communities can adapt and verify compliance.
- **Education first**: Core obligations appear in school curricula, public signage, and emergency protocols to embed norms before crises occur.

## Metrics of success
- Declining rates of cross-border harm incidents per capita (physical, digital, ecological).
- Increased treaty compliance scores and transparency indices across regions.
- Shorter resolution times for disputes with higher victim satisfaction in restorative outcomes.
- Measurable preservation of commons assets (e.g., stable atmospheric emissions trajectories, reduced orbital debris growth).
