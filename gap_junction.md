# Gap Junction Overview

Gap junctions are specialized intercellular channels that directly connect the cytoplasm of neighboring cells, allowing ions and small molecules to pass between them. They enable rapid, coordinated signaling in tissues where synchronized activity is essential.

## Structure and Assembly
- **Connexins:** In vertebrates, gap junction channels are formed by connexin proteins. Six connexins assemble into a hemichannel (connexon).
- **Docking:** Connexons from adjacent cells align to create a continuous aqueous pore across both membranes.
- **Permeability:** The pore typically permits molecules up to ~1 kDa, including ions, ATP, cyclic nucleotides, and metabolites.

## Core Functions
- **Electrical coupling:** Enables rapid spread of membrane potentials in cardiac and smooth muscle.
- **Metabolic sharing:** Allows transfer of nutrients, second messengers, and metabolites to balance cellular states.
- **Developmental coordination:** Supports patterning and differentiation by distributing signaling cues across cell groups.
- **Homeostasis:** Buffers stress by sharing protective molecules or dissipating ionic imbalances.

## Regulation
- **Phosphorylation:** Kinase activity can open or close channels by modifying connexins.
- **pH and calcium:** Low intracellular pH or elevated calcium often closes junctions to protect cells during injury.
- **Turnover:** Connexins have short half-lives; channels are continually assembled, internalized, and degraded.

## Clinical and Research Notes
- **Cardiac arrhythmias:** Altered connexin expression or distribution can disrupt electrical conduction.
- **Hearing loss:** Mutations in connexins (e.g., GJB2/connexin 26) are a common cause of inherited deafness.
- **Cancer biology:** Gap junctions can suppress or promote tumor progression depending on context and signaling milieu.
- **Therapeutic targeting:** Modulators of gap junctions are explored for ischemia, neuropathic pain, and wound healing.

## Related Concepts
- **Pannexins:** Structurally similar channels that primarily form single-membrane pores for ATP release rather than classic gap junctions.
- **Tunneling nanotubes:** Longer-range cytoplasmic bridges that permit organelle or protein transfer.
- **Electrical synapses:** Neuronal gap junctions that allow fast, bidirectional synaptic transmission.
