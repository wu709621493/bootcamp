# War Velocity by Year

"War velocity" can mean how quickly military systems move, decide, and strike. There is no single global metric, so the table below uses a practical proxy: **dominant battlefield movement/engagement speed** in each period, plus the operational change that increased tempo.

| Year (approx.) | Typical dominant military velocity | Operational shift in war tempo |
|---|---:|---|
| 1805 | Infantry columns and cavalry: ~4–15 km/h sustained movement | Napoleon-era corps maneuver improved campaign speed through distributed logistics. |
| 1863 | Rail-enabled force movement: ~25–40 km/h (train speed) | Railways began moving armies faster than foot logistics, shrinking mobilization windows. |
| 1916 | Early tanks: ~6–8 km/h; artillery remained positional | Industrial firepower outpaced maneuver, producing slow front lines despite mechanization attempts. |
| 1939 | Motorized blitz spearheads: ~30–50 km/h road advance rates (situational) | Combined arms + radio command sharply accelerated breakthrough exploitation. |
| 1944 | Strategic bombers: ~350–500 km/h | Air power expanded war tempo from front-line battles to continuous theater-wide strikes. |
| 1952 | Early jet combat aircraft: ~900–1,100 km/h | Reaction times compressed; air defense and interception became minute-scale contests. |
| 1973 | Precision-guided strike systems + integrated SAM networks | Kill chains became sensor-led rather than solely massed-fire led. |
| 1991 | Air-ground networked operations with near-real-time ISR | OODA loops shortened through satellite/GPS-enabled command and precision coordination. |
| 2003 | High-speed ground thrusts + persistent airborne surveillance | Fast maneuver combined with continuous targeting, reducing operational pause durations. |
| 2022 | Drone-mediated tactical loops (seconds to minutes) | Cheap ISR/strike drones and digital comms compressed platoon-level decisions dramatically. |
| 2024 | Human-machine fused targeting with AI-assisted cueing | Multi-domain conflict velocity increasingly set by data-to-decision latency, not platform speed alone. |

## Key Trend

Across two centuries, war velocity shifted from **platform speed** (horses, rails, engines) to **decision speed** (sensing, processing, targeting, and adapting). In current conflicts, the side that updates the kill chain fastest often gains more than the side with the fastest vehicle.
