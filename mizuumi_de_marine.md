# Mizuumi de Marine

On a lake without salt the sailors practiced tides of their own making, rowing to the metronome of distant waves they could only imagine.
The water was glassy, indifferent to anchors or storms, so they learned to invent currents with stories and songs.
They charted constellations reflected on the surface, maps that would vanish with the next ripple and demand to be traced again.
Every oar stroke was a rehearsal for courage: not against danger, but against the boredom that corrodes attention.
They found that marine is not a location but a discipline—a promise to steward whatever body of water holds you, inland or open sea.
So the lake became their ocean: small, bounded, yet deep enough for wonder to sound like a foghorn in the morning mist.
