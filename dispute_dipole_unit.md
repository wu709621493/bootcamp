# Dispute Dipole Unit

A **Dispute Dipole Unit (DDU)** is a compact framework for resolving conflicts by modeling disagreement as two legitimate poles with a negotiable field in between. Instead of treating one side as “right” and the other as “wrong,” the unit treats each side as carrying information, risk, and value.

## Core idea

A dipole has two ends; a dispute has two perspectives. The DDU asks both parties to define:

1. **Need**: What outcome is non-negotiable?
2. **Fear**: What failure must be avoided?
3. **Proof**: What evidence would change your view?

The overlap between these answers becomes the working zone for settlement.

## Five-part unit structure

- **Pole A statement**: one paragraph in plain language.
- **Pole B statement**: one paragraph in plain language.
- **Shared facts ledger**: agreed evidence, timestamped and source-linked.
- **Risk symmetry table**: short matrix showing harms to each side under each option.
- **Decision clock**: bounded timeline with checkpoints and escalation path.

## Operating protocol

1. **Mirror pass**: each side restates the other side’s position until acknowledged as accurate.
2. **Evidence pass**: claims are tagged as factual, interpretive, or value-based.
3. **Option pass**: generate at least three options, including one hybrid option.
4. **Stress pass**: test each option against worst-case scenarios.
5. **Commit pass**: choose the option with the highest joint survivability.

## Output format

A completed DDU produces:

- a one-page resolution memo,
- explicit owner assignments,
- measurable success criteria,
- and a re-open condition if assumptions fail.

## Why publish this unit

Disputes often escalate because process is vague. Publishing a clear unit helps teams, communities, and institutions resolve conflict quickly, document tradeoffs, and reduce repeated argument cycles.
