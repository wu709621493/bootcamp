# Fullerene

Fullerenes are carbon-only molecules where atoms form closed cages made from pentagons and hexagons. Their most famous member is C60, shaped like a truncated icosahedron reminiscent of a soccer ball, but the family spans many sizes and symmetries.

## Structural features
- **Cage topology:** Each carbon is sp2-hybridized and bonds to three neighbors, creating a curved conjugated network that satisfies the isolated pentagon rule in the most stable species.
- **Diversity:** Structures range from small cages such as C20 to giant endohedral clusters that trap metal ions (e.g., M@C82) or other atoms inside the carbon shell.
- **Reactivity:** High curvature raises pyramidalization strain, making double bonds more reactive than in planar graphene. [6,6] bonds across adjacent hexagons are typical sites for addition reactions.

## Key properties
- **Electronic:** Delocalized π-electrons provide tunable band gaps; pristine C60 behaves as a semiconductor, while alkali-doped variants can become metallic or superconducting.
- **Photophysical:** Strong absorption in the UV-visible and efficient intersystem crossing yield long-lived triplet states, enabling photodynamic and singlet-oxygen generation chemistry.
- **Mechanical:** The closed-shell geometry gives high resilience against compression; when aggregated, fullerene crystals pack in fcc lattices with modest van der Waals cohesion.

## Synthesis and processing
- **Arc discharge & laser ablation:** Vaporizing graphite in a helium atmosphere followed by rapid quenching forms a mixture of fullerenes that can be extracted with nonpolar solvents.
- **Combustion routes:** Flame reactors tuned for low oxygen and high temperature produce grams-to-kilograms of C60/C70 at lower cost.
- **Purification:** High-performance liquid chromatography separates species by size and shape. Functionalization (e.g., Bingel cyclopropanation) improves solubility for solution processing.

## Applications
- **Organic electronics:** Fullerene derivatives such as PCBM serve as electron acceptors in organic photovoltaics and as n-type layers in organic field-effect transistors.
- **Medicine and biology:** Water-soluble conjugates show promise as MRI contrast enhancers, antiviral agents, and reactive oxygen scavengers, though toxicity profiles depend strongly on surface chemistry.
- **Materials:** Endohedral metallofullerenes offer stable single-molecule magnets; fullerene-filled nanotubes (“peapods”) modify mechanical and electronic behavior of the host tube.
- **Energy storage:** Redox-active cages and conductive derivatives are explored for battery electrodes and supercapacitors.

## Challenges and research directions
- Scaling synthesis while maintaining monodispersity and low cost remains a bottleneck.
- Better control of regioselective functionalization is needed to tailor optoelectronic properties reproducibly.
- Long-term environmental and biological impacts of fullerene derivatives require thorough study before widespread deployment.
