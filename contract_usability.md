# Usability of Contracts

A contract is more usable when people can understand, navigate, and execute it without specialized support. The following practices improve usability for both digital and paper agreements:

- **Plain language**: Use short sentences, avoid jargon, and define required terms up front so parties know their rights and duties immediately.
- **Structured layout**: Apply clear headings, numbered sections, and consistent formatting for obligations, timelines, payments, and remedies. Include a front-page summary of the key commercial points.
- **Action-focused clauses**: Describe who must do what, by when, and under which conditions. Pair obligations with measurable outcomes and clear acceptance criteria.
- **Standardized templates**: Reuse vetted clause libraries for common items (confidentiality, service levels, termination, dispute resolution) to reduce errors and speed negotiation.
- **Checklists and tables**: Present inputs such as deliverables, milestones, pricing, and service-level targets in tables and checklists to minimize ambiguity and transcription mistakes.
- **Version and change control**: Track edits with version numbers and change logs; prefer digital signing with audit trails to ensure everyone works from the same version.
- **Accessibility**: Ensure documents are readable on mobile and desktop, offer alt text for images, and follow accessibility standards so all parties can review the contract.
- **Automation hooks**: Where appropriate, align clause wording with system fields (dates, amounts, triggers) so obligations can be monitored automatically or mirrored in smart-contract code.
- **Testing usability**: Pilot the contract with representative users, noting where they pause or misinterpret clauses, then iterate language and layout accordingly.

Applying these usability steps keeps contracts easier to read, negotiate, and execute, reducing downstream disputes and support costs.
