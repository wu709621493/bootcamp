# Luster

Luster is the quiet promise that a surface can still speak. It is not raw brightness; it is the way light is received, filtered, and returned with character. A pearl glows because it layers patience. A well-used tool gleams because it has been held, honed, and honored.

Luster depends on care. Dust dulls it, neglect mutes it, and haste scratches it. The simplest ritual—wiping a mirror, polishing a stone, tending to one small thing—restores the language of reflection. In that way, luster is the art of maintenance: a reminder that attention accumulates.

There is a human luster, too. It appears when someone carries themselves with quiet clarity, when their words are consistent, when their presence eases the room. It is less about dazzling and more about steadiness, the soft radiance of trust built over time.

To cultivate luster is to respect the surfaces we move through—skin, wood, metal, memory—and to leave them better than we found them. It is a modest brilliance, earned and shared.
