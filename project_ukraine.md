# Project Ukraine

## Purpose
Project Ukraine outlines a collaborative framework to accelerate Ukraine's recovery, resilience, and modernization. It focuses on rebuilding critical systems, strengthening democratic institutions, and positioning communities to thrive through transparent partnerships with local stakeholders and international allies.

## Guiding principles
1. **Local leadership**: Empower Ukrainian municipal and regional authorities to co-design plans and manage funds with clear accountability.
2. **Rule-of-law alignment**: Anchor all investments in transparent procurement, anti-corruption safeguards, and judicial independence.
3. **Security-by-design**: Integrate civil defense, cyber resilience, and critical infrastructure protection into every initiative.
4. **People-centered recovery**: Prioritize housing, healthcare, education, and livelihoods with attention to displaced and vulnerable populations.
5. **Green modernization**: Rebuild using low-emission energy, efficient buildings, and climate-adaptive land use.
6. **Open partnerships**: Coordinate openly with international donors, private capital, and civil society to avoid duplication and maximize impact.

## Focus areas
- **Energy and grid resilience**: Repair and harden transmission lines, expand distributed renewables, and deploy smart-grid controls to improve reliability.
- **Housing and community reconstruction**: Retrofit damaged housing, build energy-efficient community centers, and co-develop urban plans with residents.
- **Critical transport and logistics**: Restore rail hubs, roads, and bridges with redundant routes, digital freight tracking, and customs streamlining to support exports.
- **Digital and cyber infrastructure**: Harden government and utility networks, expand secure connectivity for schools and clinics, and promote digital ID adoption with privacy safeguards.
- **Healthcare and rehabilitation**: Rebuild clinics, increase mental health services, and scale prosthetics and rehabilitation programs for civilians and veterans.
- **Agriculture and demining**: Clear contaminated farmland, introduce precision agriculture tools, and improve grain storage and export terminals.
- **Education and workforce**: Modernize vocational programs in construction, energy, IT, and healthcare; provide scholarships for displaced students; and expand language and entrepreneurship training.
- **Governance and civil society**: Support open-budget portals, participatory budgeting pilots, investigative journalism, and community watchdog groups.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Establish a national recovery coordination council with municipal representation to prioritize projects and align funding sources.
2. Publish transparent project pipelines with cost estimates, expected outcomes, and procurement timelines accessible to the public.
3. Launch pilot corridors in three oblasts combining energy upgrades, transport restoration, housing reconstruction, and digital services.
4. Stand up a secure data platform for monitoring progress, managing grants, and collecting community feedback in Ukrainian and regional languages.
5. Expand successful pilots nationwide with annual external audits, public scorecards, and parliamentary oversight hearings.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Corruption or fund misuse**: Implement e-procurement, real-time expenditure dashboards, and whistleblower protections; require third-party audits.
- **Security threats**: Integrate hardened shelters, redundant power and communications, and cyber incident response playbooks across projects.
- **Community displacement**: Use inclusive design reviews, fair compensation mechanisms, and housing-first policies for displaced families.
- **Environmental harm**: Mandate impact assessments, mine-clearing protocols, and nature-based solutions to manage flood and erosion risks.
- **Donor fatigue or coordination gaps**: Maintain shared standards, publish unified reporting, and convene quarterly coordination forums.

## Success metrics
- Percentage of reconstructed housing units meeting energy-efficiency standards and equipped with shelters.
- Reduction in power outages and increase in renewable generation capacity connected to the grid.
- Kilometers of repaired or newly redundant rail and road corridors with improved transit times.
- Number of healthcare facilities rebuilt and patients served in mental health and rehabilitation programs.
- Hectares of farmland cleared of mines and brought back into production, with improved yield metrics.
- Uptake of digital public services, procurement transparency scores, and community satisfaction ratings.
