# Project Tokyo

## Purpose
Project Tokyo outlines a climate-resilient and human-centered roadmap for the metropolis that balances economic dynamism, cultural heritage, and livability. The plan focuses on climate adaptation, inclusive mobility, and neighborhood-scale services that keep Tokyo competitive while improving daily quality of life.

## Guiding principles
1. **Climate-ready urbanism**: Design for heat, flooding, and seismic resilience while accelerating decarbonization.
2. **Neighborhood vitality**: Strengthen 15-minute districts with healthcare, childcare, and daily retail within walking distance.
3. **Inclusive mobility**: Expand safe, affordable, and accessible transit and active travel for all ages.
4. **Digital public services**: Modernize civic services with privacy-first, multilingual tools and open data.
5. **Cultural continuity**: Protect historic streetscapes and promote local creative industries.

## Focus areas
- **Heat and flood adaptation**: Expand green corridors, cool roofs, permeable surfaces, and floodable parks along river basins.
- **Energy transition**: Scale building retrofits, district energy pilots, and rooftop solar paired with storage.
- **Transit modernization**: Improve last-mile connectivity, station accessibility, and night-time transit coverage.
- **Affordable housing**: Preserve mixed-income neighborhoods and repurpose underused commercial space for housing.
- **Public health and aging**: Expand community clinics, eldercare hubs, and social connection programs.
- **Innovation economy**: Support startup corridors, university-industry labs, and circular manufacturing clusters.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Establish a cross-ward resilience office to coordinate climate adaptation, infrastructure, and emergency planning.
2. Map heat and flood risks at the neighborhood scale and prioritize interventions for vulnerable communities.
3. Launch rapid retrofits for public buildings and incentives for private building energy upgrades.
4. Pilot 15-minute district upgrades around key stations with safe streets, public space, and service access.
5. Expand open data platforms, civic APIs, and multilingual digital services.
6. Publish annual progress dashboards and independent audits with community feedback loops.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Heat island amplification**: Deploy urban tree canopies, cool pavements, and reflective materials at scale.
- **Flood and storm surge**: Upgrade drainage, expand green infrastructure, and strengthen coastal defenses.
- **Demographic shifts**: Align housing and healthcare investments with an aging population and workforce needs.
- **Economic headwinds**: Diversify revenue with innovation districts and resilience-focused infrastructure jobs.
- **Equity gaps**: Target subsidies, accessibility upgrades, and service coverage to underserved wards.

## Success metrics
- Square meters of cool roof coverage and reductions in heat-related hospitalizations.
- Megawatts of distributed clean energy and building retrofit completion rates.
- Transit accessibility scores, last-mile travel times, and active mobility adoption.
- Affordable housing units preserved or created near transit hubs.
- Community clinic capacity growth and eldercare program participation.
- Startup formation rates and circular economy job growth.
