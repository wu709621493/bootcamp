# Crude oil, cruel oil

Crude oil is a reservoir of ancient sunlight and pressure, a compressed archive of organisms that never met us yet now power our commutes. That inheritance is convenient, but it also runs on a clock of extraction that keeps resetting our sense of urgency. The more wells we drill, the more we act as if the past was meant to underwrite every present-day delay in building cleaner systems.

The cruelty is not just in spills or carbon numbers; it is in the way dependence reorganizes our choices. Pipelines redraw maps, subsidies harden habits, and distant refineries dictate the air quality of towns that never signed up for the deal. People living near flares breathe the costs each night, while the benefits are wired into stock tickers and shipping manifests.

Breaking the pattern demands more than swapping fuels. It means refusing to call extraction inevitable, investing in jobs that do not rely on combustion, and designing cities that treat short trips as walking distances instead of micro-burns of petroleum. The most humane thing we can do with crude oil is to let it stay ancient—an archive we read, not a resource we endlessly burn.
