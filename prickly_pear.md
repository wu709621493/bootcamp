# Prickly Pear: Cactus Fruit with Floral Sweetness

Prickly pear (tuna) is the jewel-toned fruit of the opuntia cactus. Its flavor leans toward melon and berry with a whisper of cucumber, and its magenta juice turns everything from agua fresca to vinaigrette a bright pink.

## Buying and Storing
- Choose fruit that yields slightly to pressure and has a uniform skin color; rock-hard fruit is underripe.
- Avoid deep cuts or mold at the stem end, which can signal internal decay.
- Refrigerate whole fruit for up to a week; peeled fruit can be frozen for smoothies or sorbet.

## How to Prep Safely
1. Wear gloves or use tongs to avoid the tiny, hair-like glochids on the skin.
2. Rinse the fruit under running water and scrub gently to remove any remaining spines.
3. Slice off the ends, score the skin lengthwise, and peel it away from the flesh.

## Essential Uses
- **Agua fresca:** Blend peeled fruit with cold water, lime juice, and a pinch of salt; strain out the seeds.
- **Jam:** Simmer pulp with sugar and lemon until glossy and thick; the seeds soften but remain crunchy.
- **Salsa:** Dice with tomato, red onion, cilantro, and jalapeño for a sweet-heat topper.
- **Cocktails:** Shake juice with tequila, citrus, and agave for a cactus margarita.

## Flavor Pairings
- Citrus, mint, hibiscus, and basil
- Chile powder, tajín, and smoked salt
- Soft cheeses like ricotta or goat cheese

## Safety Notes
- The seeds are edible but hard; strain or blend thoroughly if you prefer a smoother texture.
- Handle peel and scraps carefully; discard in a sealed bag to avoid accidental contact with spines.
