# One-arm Chopin competition

## Prelude: a different kind of virtuosity

A one-arm Chopin competition reframes the meaning of virtuosity from reach to resourcefulness. The performer does not shrink the composer; instead, they reimagine the score’s architecture so a single hand can still carry the nobility, melancholy, and light of Chopin’s writing. The competition celebrates the ability to keep the music’s emotional core intact while honoring the physical truth of the performer.

## Principles of adaptation

- **Sing the line.** Chopin’s music is defined by long, lyrical lines. A one-hand transcription should prioritize the melodic voice, even if the accompaniment must be simplified or redistributed.
- **Redistribute harmony.** Use rolled chords, arpeggiations, and strategic voicing to imply the harmonic field that two hands would normally sustain.
- **Preserve rubato.** Timing is where Chopin breathes; rubato becomes a compensatory technique for limited span, letting the line expand and contract with expressive intent.
- **Respect the original texture.** Adaptations should sound like Chopin, not like a technical exercise. The listener should feel continuity with the original repertoire.

## Competition structure

1. **Set piece**: a required one-hand transcription approved by the jury, demonstrating fidelity to Chopin’s harmonic language.
2. **Free choice**: a one-hand arrangement selected by the pianist, showing personal affinity and interpretive courage.
3. **Original transcription**: a short Chopin excerpt arranged by the competitor, evaluated for musical integrity, practicality, and inventiveness.

## Judging criteria

- **Musical continuity**: Does the adaptation preserve the spirit of Chopin’s voice-leading and phrasing?
- **Technical ingenuity**: Does the performer create the illusion of a fuller texture without compromising clarity?
- **Narrative control**: Is there a coherent emotional arc, even in reduced textures?
- **Respectful transformation**: Does the adaptation illuminate Chopin rather than diminish him?

## Why it matters

A one-arm Chopin competition is not a novelty; it is a statement of inclusion in the classical canon. It affirms that artistry is not confined by traditional technique, and that the piano can still sing when it is asked to do more with less. It honors resilience, but more importantly, it honors imagination.
