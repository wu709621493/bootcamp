# Immune

Immunity is a layered conversation between the body and the world. Cells negotiate with signals, recall past conflicts, and decide when to tolerate and when to fight. The process is precise and improvisational at once, built on memory yet responsive to the unexpected.

## Innate readiness
- **Physical barriers** form the opening defense, blocking pathogens before the debate can begin.
- **Sentinel cells** like macrophages patrol tissues, recognizing patterns that hint at danger rather than targeting individuals.
- **Inflammation** is a rallying call: swelling and heat signal the need for reinforcements and redirect local resources toward containment.

## Adaptive memory
- **B cells** generate bespoke antibodies, refining their designs through iterative mutations that balance strength with safety.
- **T cells** survey the body's internal displays, removing compromised cells while preserving healthy function.
- **Memory populations** archive lessons from previous infections, shortening response times when similar threats reappear.

## Balance and repair
- **Regulatory circuits** temper overreactions, preventing damage from surpassing the original threat.
- **Tolerance** acknowledges that not all foreign visitors are enemies; gut microbes and vaccines reshape what the immune system calls home.
- **Recovery** completes the cycle, clearing debris and restoring equilibrium so the next signal can be interpreted clearly.

Immunity is not invincibility. It is the ongoing practice of distinguishing harm from coexistence, of repairing breaches, and of remembering without overreacting. The stronger the memory and the gentler the restraint, the more resilient the organism becomes.
