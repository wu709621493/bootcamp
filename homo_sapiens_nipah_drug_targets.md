# Homo sapiens – Nipah virus interaction: drug-target map

This note summarizes *interaction points* between human host biology (Homo sapiens) and Nipah virus (NiV) and maps them to potential **drug-target classes**. It is intended for research triage and hypothesis generation, **not** for clinical decision-making.

## Interaction map (high-level)

| Stage | Virus factor (NiV) | Human factor(s) | Interaction / role | Drug-target class (examples of modality) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attachment | G glycoprotein | **Ephrin-B2 (EFNB2)**, **Ephrin-B3 (EFNB3)** | Viral attachment to receptor | Entry-blocking biologics (neutralizing antibodies to G), receptor decoys, small-molecule PPI inhibitors |
| Fusion | F glycoprotein | Host membrane lipids; endosomal processing | Membrane fusion after F activation | Fusion inhibitors (peptides/small molecules), F-protein–targeted antibodies |
| F activation | F glycoprotein (cleavage) | **Cathepsin L (CTSL)** and endosomal processing machinery | Proteolytic activation of F | Host-directed protease inhibitors; endosomal acidification modulators |
| Transcription/replication | L (polymerase), N (nucleocapsid), P (cofactor) | Host nucleotide pools; chaperones | Viral RNA synthesis | Direct-acting antivirals (polymerase inhibitors), host nucleotide synthesis inhibitors |
| Assembly/budding | M (matrix) | ESCRT machinery, membrane trafficking | Virion assembly and release | Host-directed inhibitors of budding/trafficking pathways |
| Immune evasion | V/W/P proteins | **STAT1/STAT2**, interferon signaling | Suppression of innate immunity | Immunomodulators (boost interferon responses) |

## Candidate host targets (human genes/proteins)

Below are human genes/proteins that are *commonly cited* for NiV entry or replication steps and therefore represent **host-directed target classes**. These are *not* recommendations to treat patients and require rigorous validation.

- **EFNB2 / EFNB3**: Primary entry receptors for NiV G. Targeting the G–ephrin interaction can block attachment/entry.
- **CTSL (Cathepsin L)**: Implicated in F protein processing; inhibition may reduce fusion competency.
- **Endosomal acidification machinery**: Acidic endosomes are often required for protease activation; modulators could reduce viral entry.
- **Interferon signaling (STAT1/STAT2 pathways)**: NiV V/W/P antagonize these pathways; boosting innate responses could be protective.
- **ESCRT and membrane trafficking factors**: Potentially required for assembly/budding of enveloped viruses.

## Viral target classes (direct-acting)

- **G glycoprotein (attachment)**: Neutralizing antibodies and receptor decoys.
- **F glycoprotein (fusion)**: Fusion-inhibiting peptides/small molecules; antibody neutralization.
- **L polymerase (replication)**: Nucleoside or non-nucleoside inhibitors aimed at RdRp.
- **N/P complex (replication complex stabilization)**: Small molecules that disrupt nucleocapsid assembly.

## Notes & limitations

- This map is a **conceptual framework**. Specific drug candidates and clinical efficacy are outside scope.
- Host-directed strategies may reduce viral replication but risk **off-target toxicity**.
- NiV biology is evolving; experimental verification is required for each target.
