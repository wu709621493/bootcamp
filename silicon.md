# Silicon

Silicon (Si) is a tetravalent metalloid that sits below carbon on the periodic table and underpins much of modern technology and geology. Its abundance, chemical versatility, and semiconductor behavior make it indispensable across electronics, energy, and materials science.

## Fundamental properties
- **Atomic number:** 14
- **Atomic mass:** 28.0855 u
- **Crystal structure:** Diamond cubic (covalent network)
- **Common oxidation states:** −4, +4 (stable); +2 (less common)
- **Bandgap:** ~1.1 eV (indirect, at 300 K)
- **Melting point:** 1414 °C; **Boiling point:** 3265 °C
- **Density:** 2.33 g/cm³ (crystalline at 25 °C)

These properties give silicon a balance of electrical tunability and thermal robustness that is well-suited to integrated circuits and power devices.

## Geology and production
- **Crustal abundance:** Silicon is the second most abundant element in Earth’s crust (~27% by mass), primarily in silicate minerals (e.g., quartz, feldspars, olivine).
- **Primary extraction:** Silica (SiO₂) is reduced in electric arc furnaces with carbon to make metallurgical-grade silicon (~98–99% purity).
- **Refinement:** Metallurgical silicon is converted to trichlorosilane and subjected to the Siemens process or fluidized bed reactors to yield electronic-grade polysilicon (> 9N purity). Float-zone and Czochralski methods then produce single-crystal ingots for wafers.

## Electronics applications
- **Microelectronics:** Silicon wafers host CMOS logic, memory, and analog devices. Its native SiO₂ forms a high-quality gate oxide, enabling dense MOSFET scaling.
- **Power electronics:** Doped silicon forms high-voltage IGBTs and diodes for inverters and motor drives; silicon carbide (SiC) competes at higher voltages/temperatures but silicon remains cost-effective below a few kilovolts.
- **Photovoltaics:** Crystalline silicon (mono and multi) dominates solar cell production due to mature processes and abundant feedstock. Passivated emitter rear contact (PERC), heterojunction (HJT), and tunnel-oxide passivated contact (TOPCon) architectures push efficiency beyond 24% in production cells.
- **Photonics:** Silicon photonics leverages CMOS fabs to integrate waveguides, modulators, and detectors (often with germanium) for datacenter interconnects.

## Materials and chemical uses
- **Silicones:** Organosilicon polymers (e.g., PDMS) provide flexibility, chemical stability, and biocompatibility for sealants, medical devices, and soft lithography.
- **Glass and ceramics:** Silica network formers define the mechanical and optical properties of glass; silicate ceramics provide thermal stability for refractories.
- **Alloys:** Ferrosilicon deoxidizes steel; aluminum-silicon alloys improve castability and wear resistance in automotive parts.
- **Semiconductor doping:** Group III (B, Al) and V (P, As, Sb) dopants adjust carrier type and concentration for device fabrication.

## Environmental and sustainability notes
- **Energy intensity:** Polysilicon production is electricity-intensive; regions with low-carbon power reduce embodied emissions of electronics and solar modules.
- **End-of-life:** Silicon solar module recycling focuses on recovering glass, aluminum, and silver; reclaimed silicon wafers can be downcycled into metallurgical applications or re-melted after purification.
- **Dust and occupational safety:** Respirable crystalline silica dust can cause silicosis; workplace controls (ventilation, wet cutting) mitigate exposure.

## Emerging research directions
- **Strained silicon and SiGe channels** for enhanced carrier mobility in advanced CMOS nodes.
- **3D integration** using through-silicon vias (TSVs) and hybrid bonding to stack logic and memory.
- **Quantum devices** employing isotopically purified silicon-28 for spin qubits with long coherence times.
- **Silicon anodes** in lithium-ion batteries, leveraging high specific capacity with nanostructuring and binder design to manage volume expansion.

Silicon’s combination of abundance, process maturity, and versatile physics ensures it will remain foundational while coexisting with compound semiconductors and emerging materials tailored to specific performance niches.
