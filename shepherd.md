# Shepherd

A shepherd walks the high ridges before sunrise, counting his breath as carefully as his flock.
He knows every break in the fence, every wind that whistles through the thorn, and every
stone that could send a hoof sliding. The valley below is still asleep, but he reads the sky
like a ledger: pale blue margins, gold ink where the sun is about to sign its name.

Each sheep carries a different note in the music of the hillside. The bellwether hums like
an old kettle, the lambs chirp in the thin air, and the seasoned ewes mutter at the grass as
if debating the merits of every blade. The shepherd listens, adjusting his staff the way a
conductor lifts a baton. He guides with whistles, with soft claps, with the rustle of his coat
against broom shrubs. Authority, to him, is not a weight but a way of moving just ahead of
trouble.

There are days when storm clouds arrive without warning, dark as iron pots, and he feels the
pressure drop behind his eyes. On those days he becomes a cartographer of shelter: a boulder
here for windbreak, a hollow there for warmth, a line through the heather that avoids the
hidden bog. The flock trusts him because he earned it in winter, when the snow erased the
paths and he carried the smallest lamb inside his coat to keep it breathing.

At dusk, when the sheep settle and the hills trade color for shadow, the shepherd sits and
mends his nets of thought. He remembers his father’s hands, cracked like the ground after a
dry season, teaching him to splice rope and to keep silent unless the hill spoke first. He
dreams of futures for the lambs, of new grass after rain, of leaving the world a little safer
for wool and for children. Alone with the stars, he hums the same tune as the bellwether.
It rises, falls, and returns—a path traced by patience and care.
