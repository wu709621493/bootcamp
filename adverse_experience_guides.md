# Adverse Experience Guides

## Purpose
Adverse experiences are difficult events that can affect emotional, physical, social, or academic functioning. This guide offers practical, non-clinical steps for immediate stabilization, short-term coping, and long-term recovery planning.

## 1. Immediate Stabilization (First 24 Hours)
- Ensure physical safety first (move to a safe environment, contact emergency services if needed).
- Use grounding techniques:
  - Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.
  - Slow breathing: inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 6.
- Reduce overload:
  - Hydrate, eat a simple meal, and rest.
  - Limit distressing media and social feed exposure.
- Reach out to one trusted person and state one concrete need ("Can you stay on the call with me for 10 minutes?").

## 2. Short-Term Coping (First 2 Weeks)
- Establish a minimal daily routine:
  - Wake/sleep schedule.
  - Light movement (walk, stretch, mobility drills).
  - One manageable task per day.
- Track reactions without judgment:
  - Sleep quality, appetite, irritability, intrusive thoughts, concentration.
- Use structured emotional processing:
  - Journal prompt: "What happened, what am I feeling now, what do I need next?"
  - Time-box reflection (10–20 minutes), then return to present-focused activity.
- Ask for practical support:
  - Childcare, meal help, transportation, deadline extensions, or admin help.

## 3. Communication Framework
When discussing adverse experiences, use:
- **Facts**: What happened.
- **Impact**: How it affects functioning.
- **Request**: Specific accommodation or support needed.

Example:
"I experienced a distressing event this week. My sleep and concentration are affected. I need a 48-hour extension and a brief check-in after submission."

## 4. Workplace / School Accommodations
Possible temporary accommodations:
- Flexible deadlines or reduced load.
- Quiet workspace / remote option.
- Modified attendance expectations.
- Additional supervision or check-ins.
- Referral to counseling or employee/student assistance services.

## 5. Red-Flag Indicators for Professional Help
Seek licensed clinical support promptly if you notice:
- Persistent panic, dissociation, or inability to perform daily tasks.
- Severe sleep disruption lasting more than a week.
- Escalating substance use.
- Self-harm thoughts, hopelessness, or suicidal ideation.
- Aggressive impulses or fear of harming others.

If there is immediate risk of harm, call local emergency services right away.

## 6. Recovery Over Time (1–3 Months)
- Rebuild predictability with routine and social reconnection.
- Introduce capacity gradually (not all at once).
- Review boundaries (people, places, content, workload).
- Celebrate small indicators of recovery:
  - Better sleep.
  - Less reactivity.
  - Improved focus.
  - Re-engagement with meaningful activities.

## 7. Team or Community Response Checklist
For leaders, peers, or facilitators:
- Listen without pressing for details.
- Validate distress without overpromising solutions.
- Offer choices, not commands.
- Protect privacy and confidentiality.
- Follow up within 24–72 hours.
