# Cantonese Preservation Plan

## Purpose
Cantonese is a living language with deep cultural, historical, and artistic value. This plan outlines practical actions communities, educators, media creators, and policymakers can take to sustain intergenerational fluency and everyday use.

## Core goals (5-year horizon)
1. **Increase intergenerational transmission** so more children regularly hear and speak Cantonese at home.
2. **Expand high-quality learning pathways** for heritage speakers and second-language learners.
3. **Strengthen public visibility** of Cantonese across media, public services, and digital platforms.
4. **Preserve and share cultural assets** (oral histories, music, film, opera, slang, and local expressions).
5. **Build sustainable institutions** that continue preservation work beyond single grants or campaigns.

## Strategic pillars

### 1) Family and community transmission
- Launch community "Cantonese at Home" toolkits with age-based activities (songs, bedtime stories, games).
- Support weekend playgroups and parent-child circles where only Cantonese is used.
- Train volunteer language mentors (grandparents, retirees, university students) for family matching.
- Create neighborhood micro-grants for local conversation clubs and storytelling nights.

### 2) Education pipeline
- Develop preschool and K-12 supplementary curricula with leveled speaking/listening benchmarks.
- Offer teacher training certificates focused on Cantonese pedagogy and inclusive classroom practices.
- Build open educational resources (OER): lesson plans, graded readers, pronunciation guides, and assessments.
- Partner with universities to create minors/certificates in Cantonese language and culture.

### 3) Media, arts, and digital ecosystem
- Fund subtitling/dubbing projects for children's programming and educational videos in Cantonese.
- Establish creator grants for podcasts, short-form video, and youth-led Cantonese content.
- Build a public digital archive of oral histories, idioms, place names, and domain-specific vocabulary.
- Encourage game developers and app teams to ship Cantonese UI, voice packs, and input support.

### 4) Public services and policy support
- Advocate bilingual service access in high-need institutions (clinics, schools, legal aid, transit hubs).
- Publish plain-language public information in Cantonese (audio + text).
- Integrate Cantonese language access targets into municipal inclusion and cultural plans.
- Incentivize businesses that provide Cantonese service capacity in relevant neighborhoods.

### 5) Research, standards, and documentation
- Maintain a living corpus across spoken varieties and registers.
- Standardize practical romanization references while respecting variation.
- Support NLP and speech-tech projects (ASR/TTS, search, OCR, spellcheck, translation quality).
- Conduct annual attitude and usage surveys to guide policy and funding.

## Implementation roadmap

### Phase 1 (0-12 months): Foundation
- Form a multi-stakeholder steering committee (educators, artists, NGOs, researchers, youth representatives).
- Map current assets and gaps: schools, media channels, volunteer pools, funding sources.
- Pilot 3-5 flagship programs:
  - Parent-child language circles
  - Teacher training workshops
  - Youth digital creator challenge
  - Public-service translation sprint
- Define baseline metrics and data collection methods.

### Phase 2 (Year 1-3): Expansion
- Scale successful pilots into district-wide programs.
- Launch annual Cantonese culture and language festival.
- Grow archive and OER libraries with reusable licensing.
- Formalize partnerships with schools, libraries, museums, and tech firms.

### Phase 3 (Year 3-5): Institutionalization
- Establish a permanent Cantonese Language Resource Center.
- Secure mixed funding (public budget lines, philanthropy, membership, sponsorship).
- Publish annual impact reports and adjust strategy from evidence.
- Embed preservation commitments into long-term education and cultural policy.

## Measurement framework (KPIs)
- **Home use:** % of participating families reporting weekly Cantonese use.
- **Learner outcomes:** Enrollment, retention, and proficiency progression in programs.
- **Media reach:** Monthly active users/views for Cantonese digital content.
- **Service access:** Number of institutions offering Cantonese support and usage rates.
- **Cultural preservation:** Hours/items added to archives and public reuse statistics.
- **Workforce capacity:** Number of trained teachers, translators, and content creators.

## Governance and funding model
- **Governance:** Steering committee + independent advisory panel + youth council.
- **Operations:** Small program office coordinating grants, partnerships, and evaluation.
- **Funding mix:**
  - Government language-access and cultural grants
  - Philanthropic foundations
  - Corporate sponsorships (media/tech/community banking)
  - Community memberships and event revenue
- **Risk controls:** Multi-year commitments, transparent budgets, and external audits.

## Risk register and mitigations
- **Risk:** Insufficient teacher capacity.  
  **Mitigation:** Fast-track training + mentorship + stipend support.
- **Risk:** Fragmentation across organizations.  
  **Mitigation:** Shared calendar, common metrics, and pooled grant rounds.
- **Risk:** Low youth engagement.  
  **Mitigation:** Creator funds, gaming/esports integration, and school credit recognition.
- **Risk:** Political or funding shifts.  
  **Mitigation:** Diversify funding and secure institutional policy anchors.

## First 90-day action checklist
1. Convene steering committee and approve charter.
2. Publish an asset map and baseline survey.
3. Recruit pilot families, teachers, and creators.
4. Launch one public-facing campaign: "Speak Cantonese This Week."
5. Release first open materials pack (stories, audio, worksheets).
6. Set up a transparent dashboard for monthly KPI tracking.

## Guiding principles
- **Community-led:** Native speakers and local groups define priorities.
- **Intergenerational:** Elders and youth are co-creators.
- **Practical bilingualism:** Preservation supports social mobility, not isolation.
- **Open and reusable:** Publicly funded resources should be shareable where possible.
- **Evidence-driven:** Measure outcomes and iterate regularly.

A successful Cantonese preservation plan treats language as daily infrastructure: heard at home, taught in schools, visible in public life, and alive in the media people actually use.
