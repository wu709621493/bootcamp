# Confidence Parking

**Confidence parking** is the habit of temporarily setting aside certainty so better evidence can enter.

## What it means

- Park your strongest opinion before a hard discussion.
- Label it: “This is my current best guess, not final truth.”
- Revisit it after new data, feedback, or reflection.

## Why it helps

- Reduces defensiveness.
- Improves listening quality.
- Makes revisions feel like progress, not failure.
- Encourages teams to optimize for accuracy over ego.

## A simple 4-step loop

1. **State** your position in one sentence.
2. **Park** your confidence level (e.g., 60%).
3. **Scan** for disconfirming evidence first.
4. **Update** the confidence score and explain why.

## Practical prompts

- “What would change my mind?”
- “Which assumption is carrying most of this view?”
- “What am I not seeing because I want to be right?”

## Everyday examples

- In planning: choose a direction, but tag assumptions as testable.
- In conflict: separate intent from impact and re-evaluate after hearing both.
- In learning: treat mistakes as calibration events.

Confidence parking is not indecision; it is disciplined flexibility.
