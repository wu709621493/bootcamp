# First Principles of Refraining One’s Actions

Refraining is not passivity. It is disciplined non-action in service of a higher aim.

## 1) Clarify the Objective Function
Before acting, define what "better" means:
- Reduce harm?
- Preserve trust?
- Maximize long-term freedom?

If action does not improve the objective, refrain.

## 2) Separate Impulse from Intention
An impulse is immediate; intention is examined.
Create a pause (seconds, breaths, or a day) so that action is chosen, not discharged.

## 3) Apply the Reversibility Test
Ask:
- Is this action easy to undo?
- Are the costs asymmetric if I am wrong?

The more irreversible and uncertain the action, the stronger the case for restraint.

## 4) Respect Information Latency
Reality updates slower than emotion.
When evidence is incomplete, restraint is often the most rational temporary strategy.

## 5) Minimize Externalized Cost
Do not spend other people’s time, dignity, safety, or attention to satisfy a private urge.
Refraining is ethical when the projected cost falls on others.

## 6) Protect Optionality
Premature moves collapse future options.
Strategic non-action can preserve room to learn, negotiate, adapt, or recover.

## 7) Train Identity, Not Momentary Performance
Each action is a vote for the kind of person you become.
Refraining builds a durable identity: patient, precise, and principled.

## Practical Protocol (10-Second Version)
1. Name the impulse.
2. Name the likely cost.
3. Wait one full breath cycle.
4. Choose: act, delay, or decline.

Refraining one’s actions is the art of letting values outrank velocity.
