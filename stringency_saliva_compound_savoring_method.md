# Stringency, Saliva, and the Savoring Method

This note proposes a simple tasting discipline that treats **stringency** (the tightening sensation), **saliva** (the natural solvent), and **compound** (the flavor molecules) as a feedback loop.

## Concept
- **Stringency** signals that polyphenols or acids are binding proteins, changing mouthfeel.
- **Saliva** re-wets the palate and releases new aroma compounds.
- **Compound savoring** is the deliberate pause to let those molecules re-emerge after the tightening phase.

## Method
1. **Sip and note the clamp.** Hold the liquid briefly, noticing where the mouth tightens.
2. **Breathe out through the nose.** Allow early aromatics to register.
3. **Wait for saliva.** After swallowing, keep the mouth closed for 3–5 seconds until the mouth re-wets.
4. **Re-open and inhale.** Observe the second wave of aroma and any sweet release.
5. **Record contrast.** Write down how the first wave differs from the second.

## Why it helps
This micro-pause isolates **texture shift** from **aroma release**, making it easier to describe a drink’s structure instead of only its flavor.
