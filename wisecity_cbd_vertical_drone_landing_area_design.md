# Vertical Drone Landing Area Design at WiseCity CBD

## 1. Project Vision
Design a **high-throughput, low-noise, safety-first vertical drone landing area (VDLA)** in WiseCity CBD that supports passenger eVTOL shuttles, emergency medical drones, and logistics UAVs without disrupting dense mixed-use urban life.

### Core objectives
- Integrate air mobility into CBD transport within walking distance of metro and bus hubs.
- Prioritize public safety, redundancy, and controlled operations over raw capacity.
- Minimize acoustic, visual, and wind-downwash impacts at street level.
- Provide phased expansion from pilot operation to full commercial scale.

---

## 2. Site and Urban Integration Concept

## 2.1 Candidate placement strategy
Preferred location: upper levels of a mixed-use mobility podium, connected to a transit interchange and district energy spine.

Selection criteria:
- **Setback envelope** from schools, hospitals, and heritage structures.
- Direct connection to **fire services access road** and dedicated emergency elevator.
- Structural viability for concentrated dynamic loads.
- Existing rooftop clearances and low conflict with tower crane paths.

## 2.2 Three-dimensional zoning
- **Sky Layer (60–180 m AGL):** controlled approach/departure corridors.
- **Landing Layer (podium roof):** touchdown/launch pads and safety buffers.
- **Ground Layer:** passenger security screening, package consolidation, maintenance dock.

## 2.3 Public realm interface
- Activate ground floor with transparent operations gallery and mobility information wall.
- Separate curbside for emergency and commercial trips to avoid conflict.
- Integrate shaded pedestrian routes and weather-protected queue zones.

---

## 3. Airside Functional Layout

## 3.1 Pad configuration
Initial phase (P1):
- **2 active touchdown pads (TLOF/FATO compatible)**
- **1 standby/emergency pad**
- **2 rapid-turn staging stands**

Expansion phase (P2):
- Increase to 4 active pads + 2 staging stands by modular deck extension.

## 3.2 Recommended dimensions (conceptual)
- Design aircraft envelope: rotor span class 12–16 m.
- TLOF clear diameter: 1.0D minimum.
- FATO clear diameter: 1.5D target.
- Safety area: additional 3–5 m obstacle-free ring.

> Final dimensions must comply with the governing civil aviation authority and OEM operating manuals.

## 3.3 Vertical circulation and flows
- **Passenger flow:** check-in → security → boarding lounge → controlled airside bridge.
- **Cargo flow:** screened freight lift → automated sorting bay → dispatch stand.
- **Maintenance flow:** isolated service core with lockout boundaries.

No crossings between passenger and maintenance/cargo paths.

---

## 4. Structural and Building Systems

## 4.1 Structural scheme
- Steel-concrete composite deck with vibration-damped secondary beams.
- Local reinforcement at landing gear load concentrations.
- Blast-resistant edge barriers with frangible sections where required.

## 4.2 Fire and life safety
- Foam-water hybrid suppression around pad perimeter.
- Fire-rated separation between battery room and public occupancy.
- Dual evacuation stairs + smoke-proof emergency elevator.
- On-pad thermal imaging for battery thermal runaway detection.

## 4.3 Power and charging
- 10–20 kV utility intake with N+1 transformer configuration.
- 1–3 MW peak charging envelope in full operation.
- Mix of fast DC charging and battery swap (operator dependent).
- Microgrid compatibility: rooftop PV + BESS for peak shaving.

## 5. Digital Operations Architecture

## 5.1 UTM/U-space integration
- API-level integration with city UTM provider for slot allocation.
- Geofenced corridor enforcement and dynamic no-fly updates.
- Automated conflict detection with priority rules for emergency flights.

## 5.2 Smart sensing stack
- ADS-B/Remote ID receivers.
- Wind lidar for micro-gust prediction over urban canyons.
- Noise monitor ring at podium edge and street-level sentinels.
- AI video analytics for wildlife, debris, and intrusion detection.

## 5.3 Operations center
- Local vertiport control room with digital twin wall.
- KPI monitoring: turnaround time, pad occupancy, delay causes, noise exceedances.
- Cybersecurity segmentation between OT, IT, and public Wi-Fi domains.

---

## 6. Noise, Wind, and Environmental Design

## 6.1 Acoustic mitigation
- Curved parapet with absorbent inner lining.
- Flight procedures favoring steeper climbs over commercial blocks.
- Quiet-hour operating envelope with reduced movement cap.

Target guideline:
- Keep street-level equivalent continuous noise within locally approved urban transport limits.

## 6.2 Downwash and pedestrian comfort
- CFD-tested edge geometry to diffuse rotor wash.
- Wind screens and landscaped buffer strips at key corners.
- Temporary curb closures during high-thrust takeoff profiles.

## 6.3 Sustainability
- Low-carbon concrete and recycled steel content targets.
- Stormwater capture from deck washdown.
- Full lifecycle battery handling SOPs with certified recyclers.

---

## 7. Safety Case and Regulatory Path

## 7.1 Safety management system (SMS)
- Hazard log with bow-tie risk modeling.
- Barrier assurance plan (technical + procedural controls).
- Monthly scenario drills: rejected takeoff, lost link, fire event, medevac priority.

## 7.2 Regulatory approvals (typical sequence)
1. Concept of operations (ConOps) submission.
2. Preliminary obstacle limitation and airspace assessment.
3. Environmental and acoustic impact approval.
4. Vertiport/heliport-equivalent licensing review.
5. Trial operations under restricted schedule.
6. Full service authorization.

## 7.3 Emergency integration
- Joint protocol with WiseCity EMS, fire brigade, and police aviation unit.
- Dedicated emergency holding pad always available.
- Automatic public alert and wayfinding switch in incident mode.

---

## 8. Throughput and Service Model

## 8.1 Conceptual capacity
P1 expected envelope:
- 20–40 movements/hour (mixed fleet, weather dependent).
- 120–300 passengers/hour with 4–6 seat aircraft mix.

P2 expected envelope:
- 50+ movements/hour under mature UTM coordination.

## 8.2 Service classes
- **Urban shuttle:** CBD ↔ airport/rail superhub.
- **Premium point-to-point:** business district connectors.
- **Critical operations:** medical, security, disaster response.
- **Light logistics:** high-value parcels and urgent lab samples.

## 8.3 Ground integration
- Unified booking with metro fare wallet and MaaS app.
- Real-time multimodal wayfinding and delay rebooking.
- Dynamic curb management for last-500-meter transfers.

---

## 9. Implementation Roadmap

## Phase 0 (0–6 months): Feasibility and stakeholder alignment
- Airspace simulation, initial noise/wind studies, utility pre-check.
- Community outreach and business district consultation.

## Phase 1 (6–18 months): Pilot deployment
- Build 2+1 pad layout, limited operating hours, trial routes.
- Validate noise baselines and emergency drill effectiveness.

## Phase 2 (18–36 months): Scale-up
- Expand pad/staging infrastructure and automation.
- Introduce higher-frequency schedules and inter-vertiport network links.

## Phase 3 (36+ months): Mature CBD air mobility hub
- Full digital twin optimization.
- Integrated district logistics and emergency mobility backbone.

---

## 10. Key Risks and Mitigations
- **Regulatory delay:** early authority engagement, staged approvals.
- **Public acceptance risk:** transparent data dashboard, strict quiet hours.
- **Weather disruption:** robust alternates and passenger recovery plan.
- **Cyber risk:** zero-trust architecture and red-team drills.
- **Technology uncertainty:** OEM-agnostic charging and pad interfaces.
