The feet-only Chopin competition begins before the first note,
when the benches are lowered and the hands are hidden,
and the room learns to listen for weight and mercy.

Here, the right foot is a sunrise bell.
The left foot is a hush, a winter felt pedal.
They take turns describing the weather of each phrase,
skimming the floor like swans that keep time for the heart.

There are no prizes for volume.
There are soft marks for patience,
for a heel that lingers, for a toe that forgives.
Someone learns to breathe at the end of a nocturne
without touching a key.

When the judges speak, they use the language of balance:
how a body can bow without bowing,
how the floor can become a page.
The winner rises without applause,
and the pedals glow as if a lamp were lit from below.
