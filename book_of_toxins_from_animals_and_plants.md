# Book of Toxins from Animals and Plants

## Preface
Toxins are chemical defenses or hunting tools produced by living organisms. In this short handbook, **venom** refers to toxins delivered by bite, sting, or spine, while **poison** refers to toxins that harm when touched, eaten, or inhaled.

---

## Part I — Animal Toxins

### 1) Snake Venoms
- **Key groups:** cobras, kraits, mambas, vipers, rattlesnakes.
- **Main toxin actions:**
  - **Neurotoxins** (paralysis, breathing failure).
  - **Hemotoxins** (bleeding, clotting failure, tissue injury).
  - **Cytotoxins/myotoxins** (muscle and local tissue destruction).
- **Clinical note:** species identification matters less than urgent supportive care and appropriate antivenom.

### 2) Spider Venoms
- **Notable species:** black widow (*Latrodectus*), funnel-web spiders.
- **Typical effects:** severe pain, muscle cramping, autonomic symptoms (sweating, blood pressure changes), sometimes life-threatening neurotoxicity.

### 3) Scorpion Venoms
- **High-risk genera:** *Androctonus*, *Leiurus*, *Centruroides*.
- **Mechanism:** many scorpion toxins alter sodium channel gating, causing excess nerve firing.
- **Signs:** intense local pain, agitation, salivation, cardiovascular instability in severe cases.

### 4) Marine Animal Toxins
- **Box jellyfish:** cardiotoxic and dermatonecrotic venoms.
- **Cone snails:** conotoxins target ion channels and receptors with high specificity; paralysis may progress rapidly.
- **Pufferfish (tetrodotoxin):** blocks voltage-gated sodium channels; causes numbness, weakness, respiratory failure.
- **Blue-ringed octopus:** tetrodotoxin-mediated paralysis with preserved consciousness in some cases.

### 5) Amphibian and Other Vertebrate Poisons
- **Poison dart frogs:** batrachotoxin (sodium channel opener) causes severe arrhythmias and paralysis.
- **Toads (bufotoxins):** cardiotoxic compounds; exposure can cause arrhythmias and GI symptoms.

## Part II — Plant Toxins

### 6) Cardiotoxic Plants
- **Foxglove (*Digitalis*):** cardiac glycosides can trigger dangerous arrhythmias.
- **Oleander:** all plant parts toxic; causes nausea, bradyarrhythmias or tachyarrhythmias.
- **Lily of the valley:** glycosides with digitalis-like toxicity.

### 7) Neurotoxic Plants
- **Hemlock (*Conium maculatum*):** nicotinic toxicity with progressive paralysis.
- **Datura/Brugmansia (tropane alkaloids):** delirium, hyperthermia, tachycardia, urinary retention.
- **Aconite (*Aconitum*):** sodium channel effects causing paresthesias, hypotension, ventricular arrhythmias.

### 8) Protein and Lectin Toxins
- **Castor bean (*Ricinus communis*):** ricin inhibits ribosomes; severe GI, hepatic, and systemic toxicity.
- **Abrus precatorius (jequirity bean):** abrin, another ribosome-inactivating protein.

### 9) Hepato- and Nephrotoxic Plants
- **Pyrrolizidine alkaloid plants** (e.g., some ragworts): chronic liver injury and veno-occlusive disease.
- **Aristolochia species:** aristolochic acid nephropathy and urothelial cancer risk.

### 10) Irritant and Contact Toxins
- **Poison ivy/oak/sumac:** urushiol causes allergic contact dermatitis.
- **Euphorbia latex:** strong skin and eye irritant.

## Part III — Toxin Principles and Safety

### 11) Dose, Route, and Time
Toxic severity depends on:
1. **Dose** (how much).
2. **Route** (bite/sting, oral, dermal, inhalation).
3. **Time to treatment**.
4. **Host factors** (age, comorbidities, body mass).

### 12) First-Aid Priorities (General)
- Move away from the source and protect rescuers.
- Call local emergency services/poison center.
- Support airway, breathing, circulation.
- Avoid harmful folklore treatments (cutting, suction, tourniquets unless specific protocol).
- Bring photo/sample only if safe; do not risk additional exposure.

### 13) Hospital Management Overview
- Continuous monitoring for respiratory and cardiac compromise.
- Early decontamination only when indicated and safe.
- Targeted antivenom/antitoxin when available.
- Organ support: ventilation, fluids, vasopressors, seizure control, arrhythmia management.

### 14) Prevention
- Learn local high-risk species.
- Wear gloves/boots in fieldwork.
- Do not consume unknown plants or prepare wild species without expert identification.
- Store household plants and seeds away from children and pets.

## Closing Note
Nature’s toxins are chemically diverse and medically important. Respect for biodiversity, rapid recognition of poisoning syndromes, and evidence-based emergency care can dramatically improve outcomes.
