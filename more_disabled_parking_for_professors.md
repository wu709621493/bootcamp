# More Disabled Parking for Professors

Expanding accessible parking for professors with disabilities is a practical equity measure that improves safety, punctuality, and long-term retention.

## Why this matters
- **Legal and ethical duty:** Campus access should be barrier-free for faculty members with temporary or permanent mobility limitations.
- **Reduced fatigue and injury risk:** Longer walks from remote lots can aggravate chronic conditions and increase fall risk.
- **Teaching reliability:** Faster, predictable access supports on-time classes, labs, office hours, and advising.

## Practical implementation plan
1. **Audit current utilization** by lot, building, and time window (especially class-change peaks).
2. **Convert a defined share of nearby faculty spaces** to ADA-compliant accessible spaces where shortages are documented.
3. **Prioritize high-demand academic zones** (lecture halls, labs, and advising-heavy departments).
4. **Enable temporary accessible permits** for injuries, surgeries, pregnancy complications, and rehabilitation periods.
5. **Improve wayfinding and enforcement** with clear signage, map updates, and consistent permit checks.

## Suggested campus targets
- Accessible spaces should track **real demand**, not just minimum code compliance.
- Keep at least **1–2 accessible faculty spaces** within short walking distance of every major academic building.
- Review occupancy each term and adjust supply quickly when waitlists or repeated full occupancy appear.

## Success metrics
- Lower average walking distance from vehicle to office/classroom for permit holders.
- Fewer late arrivals attributable to parking barriers.
- Improved faculty accessibility satisfaction in climate/access surveys.

In short: adding more disabled parking for professors is not a perk; it is an access and continuity investment that helps campuses function better for everyone.
