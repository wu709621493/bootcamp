# Baby

Babies learn about the world through repeating patterns: the warmth of skin-to-skin contact, the steady rhythm of a heartbeat, and the gentle timbre of familiar voices. By paying attention to these cues, caregivers can build a predictable routine that calms both child and adult.

- **Sleep**: Dim lights and soft sounds cue rest; a short, consistent bedtime song can anchor the transition.
- **Feeding**: Responsive feeding—watching for hunger cues rather than the clock—keeps babies comfortable and helps them gain trust in their environment.
- **Play**: Slow, face-to-face play with high-contrast objects supports visual tracking and early social connection.
- **Comfort**: A steady hand on the back and quiet shushing often work better than new gadgets; babies are wired to respond to human closeness.

Small, repeated interactions add up. Each calm response teaches the baby that the world is safe, and each restful moment helps caregivers stay present for the next milestone.
