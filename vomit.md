# Vomit: quick reference

Vomit is the forceful expulsion of stomach contents through the mouth. It is a symptom rather than a standalone illness and often accompanies conditions that irritate the stomach or trigger protective reflexes.

## Common causes
- Viral or bacterial gastroenteritis
- Food poisoning or overconsumption of alcohol
- Motion sickness or migraine
- Medication side effects
- Pregnancy-related nausea
- Obstruction or other acute abdominal emergencies

## What to watch for
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, dark urine, dizziness, or inability to keep fluids down
- Blood in vomit or material that looks like coffee grounds
- Severe abdominal pain, chest pain, or a stiff neck
- Persistent vomiting for more than 24 hours (or more than 12 hours in children)
- Vomiting after a head injury

Seek urgent care if any of these signs appear.

## Self-care steps
- Stop solid foods temporarily and sip small amounts of clear fluids (oral rehydration solutions, water, or diluted juice)
- Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and dairy until symptoms improve
- Slowly reintroduce bland foods (toast, rice, bananas) once fluids stay down
- Rest in an upright or slightly elevated position
- Discuss antiemetic medication with a clinician if vomiting is frequent or disrupting daily activities

## Prevention tips
- Wash hands thoroughly before meals and after restroom use
- Keep perishable foods refrigerated and cook meats to safe temperatures
- Avoid known dietary triggers and strong odors if you are prone to motion sickness or migraines
- Stay hydrated during travel and eat smaller, lighter meals

This guide is informational and not a substitute for professional medical advice. Consult a healthcare professional for personalized care.
