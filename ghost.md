# Ghost on the Edge of Memory

A ghost drifts where every hallway remembers the footsteps it once carried. It is not a shiver or a rattle of chains; it is the soft inversion of a presence, the pause after a laugh. The ghost is the silence between breaths when someone notices the room is suddenly larger than it should be.

It watches the living rearrange furniture and routines, fascinated by the way they tidy away reminders yet leave the air filled with names. When lamps click off, it rehearses the gestures that once fit its hands, and the world briefly aligns—until light returns and the moment fades back into the walls.

Mostly, the ghost is patient. It traces constellations in dust motes, floats through drafty stairwells, and listens for the single sentence that could anchor it to a story again. Until then, it keeps the house honest, proof that every life leaves a resonance, and that even absence has weight.
