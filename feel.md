# Feel

Feeling is the nervous system's briefing on reality. It distills scattered inputs into a clear direction: move closer, step back, stay still. Good reasoning does not replace feeling—it listens to it, rechecks the evidence, and chooses the next trial.

## Notes on practicing feeling well
- **Name it quickly.** Labeling an emotion reduces its grip and turns it into data you can work with.
- **Track the body.** Pulse, breath, and posture show the first indicators of stress or ease.
- **Use a timer.** When doubt stalls you, commit to a small action for five minutes; reevaluate once time is up.
- **Share and compare.** Tell a trusted partner how something feels and ask for their read; tension often eases when perspectives match or usefully diverge.
- **Archive the sensations.** A few sentences in a journal after key events make patterns easier to spot later.

## When feeling misleads
Feelings drift from the evidence when sleep is poor, attention is split, or the problem touches an old memory. When that happens, widen the dataset: step outside, call someone, reread your notes, and take the smallest reversible step instead of a dramatic one.
