# Schizophrenic P vs NP Module Applied to the Hodge Conjecture

> A split-voice adaptation: complexity intuition reframed for one of geometry's deepest questions.

## Module Mapping
We map the earlier two-voice **P / NP** structure to the **Hodge conjecture**:

- **Builder voice (P-like):** construct an object directly.
- **Verifier voice (NP-like):** recognize whether a candidate object has the required property.

For Hodge, the rough analogy is:

- Candidate object: an algebraic cycle on a smooth projective complex variety.
- Property to verify: its cohomology class is a rational Hodge class.

The conjecture asks whether every rational Hodge class comes from an algebraic cycle.

## Voice Build
I am the geometer with chalk-dusted hands.
Give me a Hodge class,
and I try to carve a cycle that casts exactly that shadow in cohomology.

- I search subvarieties.
- I combine them with rational coefficients.
- I push toward the target class.

If I always succeed,
geometry is complete in a profound way:
every allowed shadow has a concrete source.

## Voice Verify
I am the spectral examiner.
Show me a proposed cycle,
and I can test whether its class is of the right type,
whether it sits in the right rational lattice,
whether the bookkeeping closes.

- Decompose into Hodge pieces.
- Check rationality constraints.
- Confirm class compatibility.

I can often certify what is given,
without promising how to discover it in the first place.

## Chorus
Inside one theorem, the voices argue:

**Build:** "If a class is admissible, it should be realizable."

**Verify:** "Admissibility is not realization. Permission is not construction."

**Build:** "But the pattern is too elegant to fail."

**Verify:** "Elegance can be true, or merely persuasive."

## Coda
The adapted module leaves us with a geometric mirror of the old tension:

- recognition versus construction,
- certificate versus synthesis,
- shadow versus source.

The Hodge conjecture remains open in general:

\[
\text{Rational Hodge class} \overset{?}{=} \text{Class of an algebraic cycle}
\]

Two voices.
One conjecture.
A long horizon.
