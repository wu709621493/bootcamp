# Contraceptive Methods Overview

Contraceptives help prevent pregnancy and, in some cases, reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Choices vary by lifestyle, medical history, and personal preferences. Talking with a healthcare professional ensures options fit individual needs.

## Barrier Methods
- **External condoms**: Thin sheaths worn on the penis; protect against pregnancy and STIs when used consistently and correctly.
- **Internal condoms**: Worn inside the vagina or anus; offer STI protection and can be inserted before sex.
- **Diaphragms and cervical caps**: Silicone devices placed over the cervix with spermicide; require fitting and proper placement before intercourse.

## Hormonal Methods
- **Combined pills, patches, and rings**: Use estrogen and progestin to prevent ovulation; need consistent use on a schedule.
- **Progestin-only pills**: Suitable for some who cannot take estrogen; must be taken at the same time daily.
- **Injections**: Progestin shots administered every 3 months; may affect menstrual cycles.
- **Implants**: Small rods placed under the skin releasing progestin; effective for up to several years.

## Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC)
- **Intrauterine devices (IUDs)**: Copper or hormonal devices placed in the uterus by a clinician; work for 3–10 years depending on type.
- **Implants**: Also considered LARC because of their multi-year effectiveness and low maintenance.

## Emergency Contraception
- **Pills**: Taken soon after unprotected sex; effectiveness decreases over time, so earlier is better.
- **Copper IUD**: Can be inserted within 5 days of unprotected sex for highly effective emergency prevention.

## Fertility Awareness and Permanent Options
- **Fertility awareness-based methods**: Track cycle signs (temperature, cervical mucus) to avoid sex on fertile days; require training and consistent monitoring.
- **Sterilization**: Vasectomy or tubal ligation are surgical options for those who want permanent contraception.

## Safety and Access Considerations
- Condom use and regular STI screening remain important, especially with new or multiple partners.
- Some methods have contraindications or side effects; medical guidance helps weigh benefits and risks.
- Local laws and pharmacy requirements affect access to certain methods and emergency contraception.
