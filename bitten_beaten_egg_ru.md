# Bitten & Beaten (Egg-ru)

A hush of shell, a crack of teeth, a turn of wrist. The dish begins twice—once with the first bite, and once with the whisking of the egg. Bitten marks the edge of appetite; beaten marks the patient rhythm that coaxes air into a small, pale storm.

In the pan, it takes the shape of attention. Every fold is a rehearsal for tenderness, every sizzle a reminder that softness can be earned through motion. Egg-ru is the name for this double craft: to be bitten is to be claimed by hunger, to be beaten is to be made ready for care.

If you listen, the spatula keeps time like a metronome. Eat slowly. The bruise is the bloom.
