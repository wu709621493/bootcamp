# Dolphins Math 201

## Course concept
**Dolphins Math 201** is an intermediate, story-driven math module that uses dolphin behavior and marine research scenarios to practice algebra, functions, rates, and introductory statistics.

## Learning goals
By the end of this module, students should be able to:
1. Build and solve linear equations from real-world word problems.
2. Interpret function notation in movement and population models.
3. Analyze rates (speed, growth, change over time).
4. Summarize small datasets with mean, median, and variance.
5. Use simple probability to reason about observation events.

## Unit outline

### Unit 1: Motion and distance
- Convert units (meters/second to kilometers/hour).
- Compute average speed from time-stamped observations.
- Model position with linear functions.

Example:
If a dolphin travels 1800 meters in 6 minutes,
\[
\text{speed} = \frac{1800}{6} = 300\text{ m/min}
\]
which is \(5\text{ m/s}\).

### Unit 2: Dive cycles and piecewise functions
- Represent dive/surface patterns as piecewise functions.
- Estimate total underwater time from intervals.

Example piecewise depth function:
\[
d(t)=
\begin{cases}
-2t, & 0\le t<20 \\
-40, & 20\le t<50 \\
-40 + 2(t-50), & 50\le t\le 70
\end{cases}
\]
(depth in meters; negative is below surface).

### Unit 3: Population snapshots and statistics
- Calculate central tendency from pod-size samples.
- Compare two observation days with box-plot summaries.

Sample pod sizes: 8, 10, 9, 14, 11, 10
- Mean = 10.33
- Median = 10
- Range = 6

### Unit 4: Probability in field observations
- Use independent-event models for sighting probabilities.
- Compute expected number of sightings.

Example:
If sighting probability each hour is 0.35 across 5 independent hours,
expected sightings \(= np = 5(0.35)=1.75\).

## Mini-project ideas
1. **Pod Tracker**: Build a linear or exponential model for pod count over 10 days.
2. **Dive Budget**: Use piecewise functions to estimate daily oxygen-demand cycles.
3. **Sighting Forecast**: Simulate observation outcomes with a Bernoulli model.

## Suggested assessment
- 40% problem sets
- 20% data interpretation quizzes
- 30% mini-project
- 10% reflection on model assumptions and limits

## Why this framing helps
Using dolphins as context makes abstract math tangible: motion, repetition, and group behavior naturally map to functions, rates, and statistics.
