# Project Pesticide

## Purpose
Project Pesticide outlines a coordinated plan to reduce harmful pesticide exposure while preserving agricultural productivity. The project balances public health, ecosystem resilience, and farmer livelihoods through safer chemical use, integrated pest management, and transparent monitoring.

## Guiding principles
1. **Health-first protections**: Prioritize human health, especially for farmworkers, children, and nearby communities.
2. **Integrated pest management (IPM)**: Promote prevention, biological controls, and targeted interventions before broad-spectrum chemicals.
3. **Evidence-based policy**: Use toxicology data, residue testing, and field trials to guide approvals and restrictions.
4. **Farmer partnership**: Co-design programs with growers to ensure practicality, affordability, and adoption.
5. **Environmental stewardship**: Protect pollinators, soil microbiomes, waterways, and biodiversity.

## Focus areas
- **Pesticide phase-down**: Identify high-risk active ingredients and set tiered timelines for reduction and substitution.
- **IPM training and extension**: Scale field schools, advisory hotlines, and pest forecasting to support smarter interventions.
- **Safer alternatives**: Expand access to biopesticides, resistant crop varieties, and mechanical or cultural controls.
- **Exposure reduction**: Standardize PPE, buffer zones, drift-reduction tech, and application timing guidance.
- **Monitoring and transparency**: Publish residue testing results, incident reports, and water quality indicators.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Establish a multi-stakeholder task force with farmers, scientists, public health officials, and environmental NGOs.
2. Map pesticide usage by crop and region, including incident hotspots and sensitive ecosystems.
3. Pilot IPM programs in priority regions with training, input vouchers, and peer-to-peer coaching.
4. Introduce phased restrictions for the highest-risk chemicals alongside subsidized alternatives.
5. Launch a public dashboard for usage trends, residues, and compliance metrics.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Yield concerns**: Provide adaptive trials, crop insurance incentives, and transition grants.
- **Non-compliance**: Pair enforcement with education, reporting hotlines, and supply-chain accountability.
- **Pest resistance**: Rotate modes of action and invest in predictive pest surveillance.
- **Equity gaps**: Ensure smallholders receive training, credit support, and affordable alternatives.
- **Data gaps**: Fund regional labs and standardized sampling protocols.

## Success metrics
- Reduced pesticide-related health incidents and residue levels in food and water.
- Increased adoption rates of IPM practices and biopesticide alternatives.
- Measurable improvements in pollinator abundance and soil health indicators.
- Stable or improved yields in pilot regions.
- Transparent, publicly accessible reporting on pesticide usage and compliance.
