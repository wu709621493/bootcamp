# Wall-kee-E

Wall-kee-E was built as an ambulatory seawall, a rolling mosaic of recycled hulls and solar panels. It patrolled tidal towns where climate anxiety and salt spray mixed in equal measure.

Children chalked messages on its ceramic plates and Wall-kee-E recorded every scribble, converting bright doodles into data points about happiness, air quality, and safe play space. Engineers loved the metrics; poets loved the way the wall hummed at dusk when the last ferry horn faded.

When storms surged, Wall-kee-E locked its segmented feet, anchored itself, and became a barrier against the waves. When the waters calmed, it unlocked and strolled back into the streets, lowering ramps so wheelchairs and bikes could cross without meeting a curb.

People began to follow its route for evening walks, letting the robot set the pace of the neighborhood. In doing so, Wall-kee-E turned flood defense into civic ritual: a moving reminder that protection and gathering could be the same motion.
