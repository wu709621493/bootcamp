# Nutrition basics for everyday health

A balanced nutrition plan helps stabilize energy, support immunity, and reduce chronic disease risk. The guidance below focuses on realistic daily habits rather than restrictive rules.

## Core plate framework
- **Half vegetables and fruits:** Aim for colorful variety and at least two cups of vegetables plus 1–2 cups of fruit per day.
- **Quarter protein:** Choose lean or plant proteins such as fish, poultry, tofu, beans, or lentils; target roughly 1.2–1.6 g protein per kg body weight if you are active or trying to build muscle, and 0.8–1.0 g/kg for general health.
- **Quarter whole grains or starchy vegetables:** Prefer oats, brown rice, quinoa, potatoes, or beans to provide sustained energy and fiber.
- **Healthy fats added in small amounts:** Nuts, seeds, avocado, and olive oil improve nutrient absorption and satiety without crowding out other foods.

## Daily habits
- **Hydration:** Start the day with water and aim for 2–3 liters total, adjusting for heat and activity. Tea and coffee count toward hydration when unsweetened.
- **Regular meals:** Eat every 3–5 hours to maintain stable blood glucose and prevent energy crashes.
- **Fiber focus:** Include at least 25–35 grams of fiber from vegetables, fruit, legumes, and whole grains to support gut health and cholesterol control.
- **Limit added sugars and ultra-processed snacks:** Reserve sweets for intentional occasions and choose minimally processed foods most of the time.

## Micronutrient checkpoints
- **Calcium and vitamin D:** Three servings of dairy or fortified alternatives plus sensible sun exposure or a supplement if levels are low.
- **Iron:** Pair iron-rich foods (beans, lentils, beef, leafy greens) with vitamin C sources for absorption; watch for fatigue if intake is low.
- **B12:** Essential for vegetarians and vegans via fortified foods or supplements.
- **Electrolytes:** During heavy sweating, include sodium, potassium, and magnesium from foods like bananas, potatoes, leafy greens, and lightly salted meals.

## Meal prep shortcuts
- Batch-cook a grain and a protein twice per week (e.g., roasted chicken thighs and quinoa) to mix with different vegetables and sauces.
- Keep shelf-stable staples—canned beans, tuna, frozen vegetables, eggs, and yogurt—for quick balanced meals.
- Pack snacks with a protein + fiber combo (nuts + fruit, yogurt + berries, hummus + carrots) to stay satisfied between meals.

## Reading labels quickly
- Check the **ingredient list** for short, recognizable items and limited added sugars.
- On the **Nutrition Facts** panel, prioritize: at least 3–5 g fiber per serving, <10 g added sugar, and modest sodium (<300 mg per serving when possible).
- Use the **serving size** to avoid underestimating calories, especially for snacks and condiments.

## Special considerations
- **Weight change:** Adjust portions rather than eliminating food groups; emphasize protein and fiber when in a calorie deficit.
- **Active days:** Add a carbohydrate-rich snack 60–90 minutes before exercise and a protein + carbohydrate meal within two hours after.
- **Medical conditions:** Tailor choices with a clinician or dietitian if you manage diabetes, kidney disease, celiac disease, or food allergies.

## Minimal grocery list for a week
- Produce: leafy greens, carrots, onions, bell peppers, apples or berries, bananas, sweet potatoes.
- Proteins: eggs, chicken or tofu, canned beans or lentils, Greek yogurt, canned tuna or salmon.
- Grains and starches: oats, brown rice or quinoa, whole-grain bread, potatoes.
- Fats and flavor: olive oil, nuts or seeds, herbs, spices, citrus, and low-sodium soy sauce or vinegar for quick sauces.

Small, repeatable habits—hydrating, filling half your plate with plants, and pairing protein with fiber—deliver most of the long-term nutrition benefits without rigid dieting.
