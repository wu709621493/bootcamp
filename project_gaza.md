# Project Gaza

## Purpose
Project Gaza aims to support the well-being, resilience, and recovery of communities in Gaza through coordinated humanitarian relief, sustainable infrastructure rebuilding, and inclusive economic opportunities. The plan emphasizes local partnership, protection of civilians, and long-term stability.

## Guiding principles
1. **Humanitarian primacy**: Center civilian safety, dignity, and access to essential services in every action.
2. **Local leadership**: Empower community organizations, municipalities, and professional guilds to design and oversee projects.
3. **Neutrality and transparency**: Maintain open data on aid flows, contracting, and monitoring to build trust across stakeholders.
4. **Sustainability**: Pair immediate relief with energy-efficient, water-wise, and climate-resilient reconstruction.
5. **Conflict sensitivity**: Avoid actions that exacerbate tensions, respect international humanitarian law, and ensure equitable access.

## Focus areas
- **Emergency services restoration**: Rehabilitate hospitals, mobile clinics, and trauma care with reliable power, clean water, and medical supply chains.
- **Shelter and utilities**: Rebuild housing with modular designs, solar microgrids, and desalination or water reuse where feasible.
- **Food security and livelihoods**: Expand greenhouse farming, aquaculture, and cash-for-work programs to stabilize household incomes.
- **Education continuity**: Provide temporary learning spaces, psychosocial support, and digital curricula to keep students engaged.
- **Economic inclusion**: Create small business grants, vocational training, and safe transport corridors for trade.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Establish a joint coordination hub with local councils, NGOs, and UN agencies to prioritize needs and allocate resources transparently.
2. Map critical infrastructure damage and supply routes, then phase reconstruction starting with hospitals, water systems, and schools.
3. Deploy modular clinics, mobile desalination units, and solar microgrids to bridge gaps while permanent facilities are rebuilt.
4. Launch community-led shelter programs combining local labor, prefabricated materials, and seismic-resistant designs.
5. Track progress with public dashboards, independent audits, and quarterly community consultations to adjust tactics.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Access restrictions**: Maintain pre-cleared logistics plans, diversify entry routes, and pre-position supplies.
- **Resource diversion or corruption**: Use transparent contracting, digital payment rails, and third-party audits.
- **Security incidents**: Coordinate safety protocols with neutral humanitarian actors and local leaders; stagger site work to reduce exposure.
- **Environmental strain**: Apply water recycling, waste-to-energy pilots, and debris reuse standards in reconstruction.
- **Community fatigue**: Provide psychosocial services, fair wages, and clear communication on timelines.

## Success metrics
- Reduced mortality and morbidity through restored healthcare capacity and reliable utilities.
- Percentage of households with safe shelter, electricity, and potable water within defined service levels.
- Increased local employment and small business activity supported by grants and training.
- Sustained school attendance and improved psychosocial well-being indicators among children.
- Positive community feedback captured through regular surveys and grievance mechanisms.
