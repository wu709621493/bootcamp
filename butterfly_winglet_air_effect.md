# Butterfly Winglet Air Effect

The **butterfly winglet air effect** describes how butterfly wings shape airflow to generate lift, control, and efficiency during flapping flight.

## Core aerodynamic effects

- **Leading-edge vortex (LEV):** During each wing stroke, a stable swirling vortex can form near the front edge of the wing, increasing low-pressure lift.
- **Delayed stall behavior:** Unsteady flapping lets butterflies maintain useful lift at higher wing angles than fixed wings typically allow.
- **Wingtip/edge vortex management:** The scalloped, flexible wing outline can redirect spanwise flow and alter vortex shedding, helping maneuverability.
- **Clap-and-fling interactions (in some insects):** Wings approaching and separating rapidly can accelerate surrounding air and boost force generation at low Reynolds numbers.

## Why this matters for design

Butterfly-inspired winglets and flexible wing geometries are studied for:

1. Micro air vehicles (MAVs) needing high maneuverability in low-speed flight.
2. Energy-efficient flapping drones that exploit unsteady aerodynamics.
3. Bio-inspired control surfaces that adapt shape in gusty or turbulent air.

In short, butterflies do not rely on static wing profiles alone; they use **time-varying wing motion plus flexible geometry** to manipulate vortices and improve aerodynamic performance.
