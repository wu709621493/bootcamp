# Tornado

A tornado is a violently rotating column of air that extends from a thunderstorm to the ground, capable of producing intense winds, pressure drops, and rapidly changing hazards along its path. Understanding how they form, how they are classified, and how to stay safe helps reduce risk during severe weather.

## Formation basics

- **Ingredients**: warm, moist surface air; cooler, drier air aloft; wind shear that changes wind speed and direction with height; and a lifting mechanism like a cold front or dryline.
- **Process**: shear creates horizontal tubes of rotation that thunderstorms can tilt upright. When the rotation intensifies beneath a storm's updraft, a visible condensation funnel may connect to the ground.
- **Key storm types**: supercells are the most prolific producers, but quasi-linear convective systems and landspouts can also generate weaker, shorter-lived tornadoes.

## Classification

- **Enhanced Fujita (EF) Scale**: rates tornadoes from EF0 (65–85 mph) to EF5 (>200 mph) based on observed damage indicators and degrees of damage.
- **Path characteristics**: width can range from a few meters to more than a mile; length can span from a few hundred meters to hundreds of kilometers, although long-track tornadoes are rare.

## Warning tools

- **Radar**: Doppler velocity data reveal mesocyclones and tornado vortex signatures, while dual-polarization radar can detect debris lofted by a tornado.
- **Spotter networks**: trained observers confirm visual features like funnels, debris clouds, or damage.
- **Alerts**: National and regional weather services issue watches for favorable conditions and warnings for imminent or ongoing tornadoes.

## Safety principles

- **Seek sturdy shelter**: move to a small, interior, windowless room on the lowest floor of a well-built structure. Avoid vehicles, mobile homes, and large-span roofs.
- **Protect yourself**: cover your head and neck; use helmets, mattresses, or heavy blankets if available.
- **Stay informed**: monitor official warnings, and keep a battery-powered weather radio or mobile alerts active during severe weather.

## Research directions

- Improving high-resolution numerical models to better resolve tornadogenesis and maintenance.
- Expanding phased-array radar deployments for faster volume updates.
- Integrating mobile radar, unmanned aircraft, and surface probe data to refine forecasts and warning lead times.

Understanding tornado dynamics and preparedness steps saves lives, enabling communities to respond quickly when severe storms develop.
