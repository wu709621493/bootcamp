# Scholarship vs. Scholaraid

Scholarships and scholaraid both describe financial support for learners, but they differ in intent, structure, and expectations. This note contrasts the two ideas to clarify when each term applies.

## Scholarship
- **Primary purpose:** Reward academic, artistic, athletic, or civic excellence, or support learners who meet specified criteria.
- **Typical funding source:** Universities, governments, nonprofits, or private donors with missions tied to education or merit.
- **Selection approach:** Competitive review of achievements, recommendations, and statements; often renewable if performance standards are maintained.
- **Usage expectations:** Tuition, fees, or education-related costs; rarely requires repayment.
- **Key signal:** Recognizes achievement or potential while reducing financial barriers.

## Scholaraid
- **Primary purpose:** Provide need-based relief for students whose economic circumstances block access to education.
- **Typical funding source:** Charitable foundations, mutual-aid groups, community funds, or crisis relief programs.
- **Selection approach:** Means-tested or situation-based (e.g., sudden hardship, displacement, or other emergencies).
- **Usage expectations:** Flexible support that can cover tuition, housing, food, technology, or relocation; may be time-limited.
- **Key signal:** Acts as social safety net to keep learners enrolled and stable.

## Choosing the right term
- Use **scholarship** when emphasizing merit recognition and structured, renewable awards.
- Use **scholaraid** when emphasizing rapid, need-based assistance that stabilizes a student’s situation.
- In program design, combining both tools can honor achievement while ensuring financial resilience.
