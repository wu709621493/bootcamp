# Trehalose

## Key points
- Trehalose is a naturally occurring disaccharide made of two glucose units linked by an α,α-1,1-glycosidic bond.
- It is non-reducing and relatively stable, which helps protect proteins and membranes during dehydration or heat stress in many organisms.
- Found in fungi, yeasts, plants, insects, and some bacteria; it is also used as a food ingredient and excipient.

## Biological roles
- Acts as an energy reserve in some microbes and plants.
- Provides stress tolerance by stabilizing proteins and lipid membranes during drying, freezing, or high-salt conditions.
- Contributes to desiccation resistance in organisms such as brine shrimp and certain fungi.

## Food and industrial uses
- Sweetener and stabilizer in foods; less sweet than sucrose and helps retain moisture.
- Used to protect flavor and texture in frozen or dried foods.
- Common excipient in pharmaceuticals and biologics to stabilize proteins during processing and storage.

## Safety and handling
- Generally recognized as safe (GRAS) in many jurisdictions when used in food.
- Like other sugars, excessive intake can contribute to high caloric load and should be consumed in moderation.

## Environmental considerations
- Commercial trehalose is typically produced via enzymatic conversion of starch; process efficiency and sourcing influence environmental impact.
