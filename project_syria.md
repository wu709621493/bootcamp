# Project Syria (Governance Focus)

## Purpose
Project Syria outlines a governance-centered roadmap to support inclusive recovery, institutional resilience, and accountable public services across Syria. The plan emphasizes conflict-sensitive approaches, community legitimacy, and transparent resource management.

## Guiding principles
1. **Local legitimacy first**: Anchor all initiatives in locally recognized councils, cooperatives, and civil society groups.
2. **Conflict sensitivity**: Use do-no-harm analysis, scenario planning, and feedback loops to prevent exacerbating tensions.
3. **Accountability and transparency**: Publish budgets, procurement data, and performance dashboards in accessible formats.
4. **Equity across regions**: Balance urban and rural investments, including underserved and displaced populations.
5. **Rule-of-law restoration**: Prioritize impartial justice services, anti-corruption safeguards, and rights-based service delivery.

## Governance focus areas
- **Municipal capacity**: Train local administrations on planning, budgeting, and public works maintenance with citizen oversight committees.
- **Public financial management**: Standardize open procurement templates, e-invoicing, and independent audits to track aid and domestic revenues.
- **Justice and reconciliation**: Support community mediation centers, legal aid clinics, and transitional justice dialogues that are survivor-centered.
- **Basic service stewardship**: Co-manage water, electricity, and waste services through public-utility boards that include community representatives.
- **Digital governance**: Deploy low-bandwidth civic platforms for service requests, grievance redress, and publication of bylaws and tenders.
- **Civil society resilience**: Provide compliance toolkits, safety training, and microgrants to grassroots organizations, especially women- and youth-led groups.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Map existing local governance bodies and service operators; establish a coordination forum with clear charters.
2. Co-design municipal improvement plans with public consultations and publish timelines and budgets.
3. Launch three pilot municipalities focusing on transparent procurement, grievance redress mechanisms, and asset inventories.
4. Scale successful practices through a national knowledge hub, peer exchanges, and standardized toolkits.
5. Conduct annual public scorecards and third-party audits to validate progress and adjust funding.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Fragmented authority**: Use memorandum-of-understanding frameworks and neutral facilitators to align overlapping actors.
- **Corruption or capture**: Enforce conflict-of-interest disclosures, rotation of procurement panels, and open data portals.
- **Security disruptions**: Maintain contingency plans, remote monitoring options, and community liaisons for early warnings.
- **Exclusion of vulnerable groups**: Require gender, age, and disability inclusion metrics in all program approvals.
- **Data misuse**: Apply privacy-by-design standards, data minimization, and consent protocols for all digital tools.

## Success metrics
- Percentage of municipal budgets published and independently verified.
- Reduction in procurement cycle times and increase in competitive bids.
- Uptake of grievance platforms and resolution rates within defined service standards.
- Number of community-led audits and service co-management boards established.
- Improved public perception of fairness, transparency, and service reliability in annual surveys.
