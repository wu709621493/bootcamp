# Caffeine

## Key points
- Acts as an adenosine receptor antagonist, reducing perceived fatigue and improving alertness.
- Typical effective single doses range from 50–200 mg for adults; higher amounts increase side-effect risk.
- Oral absorption is rapid (peak plasma ~30–60 minutes), with a half-life of 3–7 hours depending on genetics and habits.
- Moderate intake (up to ~400 mg/day for most adults) is generally considered safe; pregnancy guidelines recommend <200 mg/day.

## Practical use
- For daytime focus: 25–100 mg microdoses can boost alertness while minimizing jitters.
- For exercise: 3–6 mg/kg taken 30–60 minutes pre-activity can enhance endurance and perceived effort.
- For sleep-friendly timing: avoid caffeine 6–8 hours before bedtime to limit sleep onset delays.

## Interactions and cautions
- Common side effects at higher doses include anxiety, tremor, heart palpitations, and gastrointestinal upset.
- Caffeine metabolism is slowed by pregnancy, some antidepressants, and oral contraceptives; smoking can speed clearance.
- Combining with alcohol masks perceived intoxication but does not reduce impairment.
- Individuals with arrhythmia, uncontrolled hypertension, or severe reflux may need to limit or avoid caffeine.

## Withdrawal
- Symptoms (headache, fatigue, low mood) typically begin 12–24 hours after cessation and resolve within 2–9 days.
- Gradual tapering (reducing 25–50 mg every few days) can minimize withdrawal discomfort.
