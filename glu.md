# Glutamic Acid (Glu)

Glutamic acid, commonly abbreviated as Glu or E, is a polar, negatively charged amino acid that plays key roles in protein structure and cell metabolism. It contributes to salt bridges in folded proteins, serves as a major neurotransmitter in the central nervous system, and functions as a precursor for the biosynthesis of GABA and glutamine.

## Codons
- **GAA**
- **GAG**

## Notable properties
- Side chain pKa around 4.1, giving Glu its negative charge at physiological pH.
- Frequently participates in catalytic residues of enzymes due to its acid/base chemistry.
- Acts as an excitatory neurotransmitter and is tightly regulated to prevent excitotoxicity.

## Dietary and metabolic notes
- Rich dietary sources include meat, dairy, soy, and fermented products like miso.
- Widely used as a flavor enhancer in the form of monosodium glutamate (MSG).
- Central to the Krebs cycle via conversion to α-ketoglutarate, linking amino acid and energy metabolism.
