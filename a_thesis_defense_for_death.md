# A Thesis Defense for Death

The committee arrived in black robes,
which felt unhelpfully on-theme.

I adjusted my slides,
cleared my throat,
and began:

"Esteemed faculty,
my argument is simple:
Death is not the opposite of life,
but its strictest editor."

Professor Hours tapped a pen.
"Define your terms."

"Life," I said,
"is a draft that believes itself final.
Death is the margin note
that makes meaning urgent."

A cough from the back row.
A skeleton in visiting-scholar tweed
crossed and uncrossed his legs.

Slide two:
a photograph of a wilting rose
beside a child’s drawing of the same rose
made with furious, immortal crayons.

"Observe," I said,
"how endings teach attention.
If petals never fell,
we would never kneel."

Professor Mercy raised her hand.
"Are you romanticizing grief?"

"No," I replied.
"Grief is the tuition.
No one enrolls willingly,
but everyone leaves
talking about what matters."

The projector flickered,
briefly showing my grandmother
shelling peas at a kitchen table
that no longer exists.

I did not advance the slide.

"Exhibit C," I whispered.

Outside,
ambulances braided the noon traffic.
Inside,
my laser pointer trembled
over a final equation:

finitude = focus.

Professor Dust leaned forward.
"And what of injustice?
Of lives cut short,
not completed?"

I swallowed.
"Then death is not a teacher there,
but a theft.
My thesis does not praise the thief.
It only explains
why the living clutch each other
harder in the dark."

Silence wrote itself
across the room.

At last,
the chair of the department,
old as oak and kinder,
closed her folder.

"Your defense," she said,
"is provisionally accepted.
Revisions required:
call your mother,
forgive your father,
plant something you will not eat,
and stop postponing joy
until peer review."

I nodded,
tears making footnotes
of my face.

When I stepped into the corridor,
Death waited by the water fountain,
holding my coat.

"Well argued," it said,
not unkindly.

"Do I pass?" I asked.

Death smiled,
the way a deadline smiles.

"You already did,
by turning in the draft
while it was still warm."
