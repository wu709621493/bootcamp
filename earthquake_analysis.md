# Earthquake Analysis

## Tectonic Drivers
- **Plate boundaries**: Most earthquakes occur along convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries where stress accumulates until faults slip.
- **Intraplate events**: Less frequent but possible when inherited weaknesses reactivate within plates (e.g., New Madrid Seismic Zone).
- **Induced seismicity**: Human activities such as wastewater injection, reservoir loading, or geothermal operations can alter pore pressures and trigger slip.

## Measuring Earthquakes
- **Magnitude**: Modern catalogs rely on moment magnitude (Mw), which scales with seismic moment \(M_0 = \mu A D\) and avoids the saturation issues of older scales like Richter.
- **Intensity**: The Modified Mercalli Intensity scale captures site-specific shaking and damage, complementing magnitude by reflecting local soil conditions and building performance.
- **Frequency content**: Response spectra and corner frequencies inform how structures of different natural periods will respond.

## Ground Motion and Site Effects
- **Amplification**: Soft sediments lengthen shaking duration and boost amplitudes through basin resonance, while hard rock sites transmit higher frequencies with less amplification.
- **Nonlinear behavior**: Strong shaking can reduce soil stiffness (strain-softening), increasing deformations and potential liquefaction in saturated loose sands.
- **Directivity**: Rupture propagation focusing energy in the direction of slip can intensify shaking in a lobe aligned with rupture velocity.

## Cascading Hazards
- **Surface rupture** damages lifelines crossing faults (pipelines, roads, rail) and is not controllable by design alone.
- **Liquefaction** leads to differential settlement, lateral spreading near free faces, and buoyancy of buried infrastructure.
- **Landslides and rockfalls** destabilize steep terrain; rainfall and lithology are critical modifiers of slope failure probability.
- **Tsunami generation** follows large vertical seafloor displacements, with basin geometry shaping wave amplification and arrival patterns.

## Risk Evaluation
- **Hazard curves**: Probabilistic seismic hazard analysis integrates ground-motion prediction equations (GMPEs), fault slip rates, and epistemic uncertainty via logic trees.
- **Fragility and vulnerability**: Component and system-level fragility curves translate ground motion into damage states, while vulnerability functions convert damage into loss metrics.
- **Exposure**: Accurate inventories of buildings, infrastructure, and population density are required to convert hazard into risk; night/day occupancy shifts alter casualty estimates.

## Mitigation Strategies
- **Performance-based design**: Targets multiple limit states (operational, immediate occupancy, life safety, collapse prevention) using nonlinear dynamic analysis where warranted.
- **Retrofit techniques**: Base isolation, supplemental damping, column jacketing, shear wall addition, and diaphragm strengthening address different structural weaknesses.
- **Lifeline resilience**: Distributed networks (power, water, telecom) benefit from redundancy, automatic shutoff valves, flexible joints at fault crossings, and prioritized restoration plans.
- **Land-use planning**: Setbacks from active faults, liquefaction-prone zones, and landslide slopes reduce exposure; microzonation maps guide zoning and design spectra.

## Monitoring and Early Warning
- **Instrumentation**: Dense strong-motion arrays, GPS, InSAR, and borehole strainmeters improve rupture detection and finite-fault inversions.
- **Early warning systems**: Trigger on P-waves or real-time geodetic estimates to deliver seconds to tens of seconds of warning for automated actions (train braking, valve closure).
- **Crowdsourcing and IoT**: Low-cost accelerometers and smartphone networks can augment official arrays, improving coverage in urban areas.

## Preparedness and Response
- **Education and drills**: Clear guidance on Drop-Cover-Hold and evacuation routes reduces injury in high-occupancy buildings and schools.
- **Supplies and communication**: Household kits, backup power, satellite messengers, and mesh networks help bridge outages after major events.
- **Post-event assessment**: Rapid tagging of structures (green/yellow/red) and remote sensing accelerate re-occupancy decisions and resource allocation.

## Research Frontiers
- **Rupture predictability**: Statistical foreshock patterns, fault creep measurements, and lab-to-field scaling inform debate on deterministic precursors.
- **Physics-based simulations**: 3D numerical models incorporating realistic fault geometries, rate-and-state friction, and topography refine scenario shaking estimates.
- **Climate interactions**: Hydrological loading, permafrost thaw, and glacial isostatic adjustment may modulate stress states and seismicity in select regions.

## Key Takeaways
- Earthquake risk is controlled by the interplay of hazard, exposure, and vulnerability; reducing any component lowers overall risk.
- Site conditions can dominate local shaking, making microzonation and site-specific response analysis critical for urban planning.
- Investment in monitoring, early warning, and resilient infrastructure yields outsized benefits in casualty reduction and economic recovery.
