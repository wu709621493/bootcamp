# Cider: From Orchard to Glass

Cider sits between wine and beer: it ferments fruit sugars like wine but drinks with the refreshment of a lager. Balanced acidity, tannin, and sparkle reveal the apple or pear varieties behind it.

## Core Styles
- **Modern sweet**: Cold-fermented juice with added dessert-apple concentrate; low tannin, bright and easy-going.
- **New World dry**: Fermented to completion, sometimes with champagne yeast; crisp, lean, and often lightly hopped.
- **Farmhouse**: Wild or mixed culture fermentation with barnyard aromatics; rustic tannins and a hazy pour.
- **Ice cider**: Cryo-concentrated juice that ferments into dessert-wine territory with honeyed depth.
- **Perry**: Pear-based, softer acidity than apple cider with a silky finish.

## Selecting Fruit and Juice
- Blend **tannin-rich bittersweet apples** (like Yarlington Mill) with **sharp apples** (like Kingston Black) for structure.
- Avoid heavily pasteurized juice that lists sorbate; it blocks fermentation.
- If pressing at home, keep temperatures cool and oxygen exposure minimal to preserve aroma.

## Making Still or Sparkling Cider at Home
1. **Sanitize** all equipment to avoid off-flavors.
2. **Pitch yeast** (wine, ale, or cider strains) into chilled, oxygenated juice; target 12–18°C for clean fermentation.
3. **Monitor gravity** until stable for 3 days. Rack off sediment to clear.
4. For **sparkling**, prime with 5–6 g/L sugar before bottling; hold at 20°C until carbonated, then store cool.
5. For **still**, stabilize with cold-crashing and let mature for two to six weeks.

## Serving and Pairing
- Chill to **6–8°C** for bright, fizzy styles; pour farmhouse bottlings slightly warmer to open aromatics.
- Pair dry cider with **cheddar, roast chicken, or pork belly**; sweeter styles complement **blue cheese or pie**.
- Use cider in the kitchen: deglaze for pan sauces, braise onions, or mix into vinaigrettes.

## Tasting Vocabulary
- **Acidity**: The tart backbone, often like green apple or lemon peel.
- **Tannin**: Grip or drying sensation from skins and seeds; lends structure.
- **Ester**: Fruity aromas like pear drop or banana; higher in warmer ferments.
- **Funk**: Earthy or barnyard notes from wild microbes; pleasant in moderation.

## Safety Notes
- Fermentation creates pressure. Use bottles rated for carbonation, and store finished cider in a cool, dark place.
- If you see gushers, haze, or unintended sweetness in a dry cider, vent bottles and refrigerate immediately.
