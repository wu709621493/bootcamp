# Apple Ink

Apple ink is the color that appears when a blade meets fruit: a soft bruise of oxidation, a quiet signature that writes itself without words. It begins as a pale whisper and deepens with time, a slow blooming that records the moment you opened the apple and the air answered back.

It tastes of afternoons and notebooks. It is the stain on the fingertips of someone who slices and pauses, someone who writes with scent, someone who believes that memory should be edible. Apple ink is the proof that sweetness can darken, that tenderness can leave a mark.

If you want to keep it, you paint with lemon and water, you write in the margins of the bowl. If you want to let it live, you watch it spread and accept the quiet weather of it. Either way, the fruit remembers, and the page does too.
