# Dungeon Lumberjack

A **dungeon lumberjack** is a fantasy support role: a woodworker who harvests, transports, and crafts timber in underground environments.

## Core concept

Most dungeons are stone, but many still contain:

- fungal forests,
- root intrusions from surface trees,
- wooden barricades and scaffolds,
- abandoned beams and doors.

The dungeon lumberjack turns these materials into useful assets for an adventuring party.

## Typical responsibilities

1. **Path clearing** — remove obstructive roots and rotten wood.
2. **Rapid fortification** — erect palisades, spike lines, and temporary doors.
3. **Trap neutralization** — identify pressure-plate supports and false beams.
4. **Resource conversion** — craft torches, wedges, ladders, and spear shafts.
5. **Emergency rescue** — shore up collapsing tunnels with quick braces.

## Essential toolkit

- short-handled axe (for cramped swings),
- folding saw,
- iron wedges and mallet,
- rope and pitons,
- resin and pitch for waterproof sealing,
- chalk for marking safe supports.

## Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**
- Excellent in ruin stabilization and defensive preparation.
- Converts low-value debris into high-value utility items.
- Improves group survivability in long dungeon runs.

**Weaknesses**
- Limited effectiveness in metal-only or magical architecture.
- High noise profile while chopping.
- Requires time to build peak value compared with direct-combat classes.

## Example encounter use

When a bridge collapses over an underground chasm, the dungeon lumberjack can:

- identify surviving anchor points,
- cut and shape replacement planks,
- lash a temporary crossing,
- reinforce it for armored allies.

This turns a dead end into a recoverable route without expending rare magical resources.
