# Investigation on Four Ice Age Periods

This brief investigation summarizes four major ice age periods in Earth’s history. Each section covers timing, geographic extent, likely drivers, and key evidence used by researchers.

## 1) Huronian Glaciation (~2.4–2.1 billion years ago)

**Timing & duration**
- Occurred during the Paleoproterozoic Era, spanning multiple glacial episodes over roughly 300 million years.

**Geographic extent**
- Evidence suggests widespread glaciation on multiple cratons, implying near-global or at least hemispheric ice coverage.

**Likely drivers**
- The Great Oxidation Event may have reduced atmospheric methane, weakening greenhouse warming.
- Continental configurations and weathering could have lowered CO₂ and amplified cooling.

**Key evidence**
- Glacial diamictites and dropstones in Paleoproterozoic sedimentary sequences.
- Paleomagnetic indicators implying low-latitude glaciation.

## 2) Cryogenian Glaciations (Sturtian & Marinoan, ~720–635 million years ago)

**Timing & duration**
- Two major glacial intervals: the Sturtian (~720–660 Ma) and Marinoan (~650–635 Ma).

**Geographic extent**
- Deposits found on nearly every continent; some evidence indicates equatorial ice, supporting “Snowball Earth” hypotheses.

**Likely drivers**
- Breakup of supercontinents (Rodinia) may have increased weathering and CO₂ drawdown.
- Positive ice–albedo feedback could have locked the planet into a deep freeze.

**Key evidence**
- Cap carbonates immediately overlying glacial diamictites, suggesting rapid deglaciation.
- Paleomagnetic data indicating low-latitude glacial deposits.

## 3) Andean–Saharan Glaciation (~460–420 million years ago)

**Timing & duration**
- Late Ordovician into early Silurian.

**Geographic extent**
- Glacial deposits across Gondwana, spanning present-day South America, Africa, and parts of the Arabian Peninsula.

**Likely drivers**
- CO₂ drawdown linked to enhanced silicate weathering and burial of organic carbon.
- Continental positioning of Gondwana at high latitudes favored ice-sheet growth.

**Key evidence**
- Glacial tillites, striated pavements, and dropstones across Gondwanan regions.
- Marine isotope records indicating global cooling and sea-level fall.

## 4) Quaternary Glaciation (2.58 million years ago–present)

**Timing & duration**
- Began at the start of the Quaternary Period; includes repeated glacial–interglacial cycles.

**Geographic extent**
- Large ice sheets covered North America (Laurentide), northern Europe (Fennoscandian), and other high-latitude regions.

**Likely drivers**
- Milankovitch orbital variations (eccentricity, obliquity, precession) paced glacial cycles.
- Greenhouse gas feedbacks and ocean circulation changes amplified climate shifts.

**Key evidence**
- Moraines, drumlins, and glacial erratics across the Northern Hemisphere.
- Ice-core records showing CO₂/CH₄ fluctuations linked to temperature.

---

## Cross-cutting patterns

- **CO₂ and methane sensitivity:** Multiple ice ages coincide with greenhouse gas drawdowns.
- **Continental configuration:** Supercontinent positions influenced ocean circulation and weathering rates.
- **Feedbacks matter:** Ice–albedo and carbon-cycle feedbacks appear central to glacial initiation and termination.
