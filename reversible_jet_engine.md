# Reversible Jet Engine

A reversible jet engine is a turbine engine equipped with a **thrust reverser** system that redirects part of the exhaust flow forward to help slow an aircraft after landing. Instead of changing the core engine’s direction of rotation, movable structures (such as blocker doors, cascades, or clamshell buckets) alter airflow so the net thrust points opposite the aircraft’s motion.

Thrust reversers reduce landing roll distance, improve braking effectiveness on wet or contaminated runways, and reduce brake wear. They are typically used during landing deceleration and, in some aircraft, for low-speed ground maneuvering. In normal flight, reversers remain stowed to preserve engine efficiency and aerodynamic performance.
