# Sawmill

## Overview
A sawmill is a workshop where logs are transformed into lumber using mechanical saws and a sequence of material-handling steps. Modern facilities combine automation with safety-first practices to maximize yield and protect workers.

## Process Flow
1. **Log intake and debarking**: Trucks unload logs onto a deck, where they are measured, sorted, and stripped of bark to protect blades and improve board quality.
2. **Primary breakdown**: A headrig or band saw cuts the log into cants (large squared beams) based on scan data that models the highest-value yield.
3. **Edging and resawing**: Cants pass through edgers to remove waney edges, then resaws slice them into boards with target dimensions.
4. **Trimming and grading**: Precision trimmers square board ends, after which graders evaluate boards for strength and appearance, assigning standardized grades.
5. **Drying and finishing**: Kilns reduce moisture content, followed by planers that smooth surfaces and achieve final dimensions before packaging.

## Key Safety Practices
- Maintain clear lockout/tagout procedures for blade changes and jam removal.
- Use kickback guards, anti-snag skirts, and emergency stop pull-cords along conveyors.
- Provide hearing protection, cut-resistant gloves, face shields, and anti-slip footwear.
- Keep catwalks and work zones free of sawdust buildup to prevent slips and fires.

## Efficiency Considerations
- **Optimization software**: Real-time scanning optimizes cuts to maximize recovery from each log.
- **Blade maintenance**: Scheduled sharpening and tensioning reduce kerf width and energy use.
- **Energy management**: Variable-frequency drives on conveyors and saw motors reduce power spikes.
- **Waste recovery**: Bark fuels boilers, sawdust feeds pelletizers, and chips supply pulp mills, reducing disposal costs.

## Environmental Impact
Modern sawmills aim to minimize waste and emissions by integrating biomass boilers, closed-loop water systems for cooling, and continuous monitoring for particulate emissions. Sustainable sourcing certifications (e.g., FSC or PEFC) help ensure logs come from responsibly managed forests.
