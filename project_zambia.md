# Project Zambia

## Purpose
Project Zambia advances inclusive economic growth and human development across Zambia by strengthening rural services, climate-resilient infrastructure, and locally led livelihoods. The plan focuses on equity between urban and rural communities while supporting public health, education, and sustainable agriculture.

## Guiding principles
1. **Local leadership**: Empower community councils, cooperatives, and Zambian institutions to co-design and manage programs.
2. **Equity and inclusion**: Prioritize women, youth, and underserved regions in access to services and opportunities.
3. **Climate resilience**: Align investments with drought, flood, and heat adaptation needs.
4. **Transparency and accountability**: Use open procurement, community scorecards, and public reporting.
5. **Sustainability**: Build long-term capacity and maintenance plans for every asset.

## Focus areas
- **Rural health access**: Expand primary care outreach, cold-chain vaccine logistics, and maternal health services.
- **Education continuity**: Improve school infrastructure, digital learning access, and teacher support in remote districts.
- **Water and sanitation**: Scale borehole rehabilitation, solar pumping, and community-led hygiene programs.
- **Agriculture and food security**: Support climate-smart farming, storage facilities, and market links for smallholders.
- **Energy access**: Deploy solar mini-grids and clean cookstoves to reduce energy poverty and deforestation.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Convene a national-local coordination platform with ministries, provinces, NGOs, and traditional leaders.
2. Map service gaps by district, prioritizing health posts, water points, and school repairs.
3. Launch pilot clusters that bundle water, health, and energy upgrades with local workforce training.
4. Scale successful pilots through blended finance, cooperative ownership, and district maintenance funds.
5. Publish quarterly progress dashboards with community feedback loops and corrective action plans.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Financing delays**: Diversify funding partners and stage projects into modular phases.
- **Climate shocks**: Integrate drought-tolerant crops, flood-resistant designs, and emergency reserves.
- **Supply chain disruptions**: Pre-qualify local vendors and maintain buffer inventories.
- **Capacity constraints**: Provide technical training and mentorship for local implementers.
- **Governance challenges**: Use transparent reporting, audits, and grievance mechanisms.

## Success metrics
- Increased percentage of rural households with access to safe water and basic sanitation.
- Reduced travel time to primary health services and improved maternal health outcomes.
- Improved school attendance and completion rates in targeted districts.
- Higher smallholder yields and post-harvest loss reductions.
- Growth in renewable energy access and reduced reliance on biomass fuels.
