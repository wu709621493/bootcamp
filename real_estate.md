# Real Estate as a Civic System

A concise guide to treating land, housing, and the built environment as long-term public infrastructure.

## 1) Purpose and Scope
- Provide stable, safe housing while preserving mobility for workers and families.
- Balance private investment with the public costs of infrastructure, climate risk, and displacement.
- Treat land as a finite resource that requires careful stewardship, not just speculation.

## 2) Market Health Principles
- **Transparency over opacity.** Publish comparable sales, vacancy rates, and permitting timelines in a standardized, open format.
- **Durable affordability.** Pair new supply with long-term affordability instruments (community land trusts, deed restrictions, shared equity).
- **Risk-aligned pricing.** Reflect climate exposure, insurance volatility, and infrastructure costs in pricing and underwriting.
- **Anti-flip friction.** Use graduated transfer taxes or minimum holding periods to deter pure speculation.

## 3) Housing Supply Strategy
- Allow a full range of housing types: ADUs, duplexes, small apartment buildings, and mixed-use corridors.
- Pre-approve design templates for fast, predictable permitting without sacrificing safety.
- Incentivize adaptive reuse of underutilized commercial and industrial assets.

## 4) Equity and Tenant Stability
- Expand right-to-counsel and clear notice requirements for eviction processes.
- Fund relocation support and anti-displacement measures in rezoning areas.
- Protect renters with transparent lease terms and limits on hidden fees.

## 5) Infrastructure and Climate Alignment
- Tie new development to transit, utilities capacity, and emergency response coverage.
- Require climate-resilient construction in flood, fire, and heat-risk zones.
- Invest in green infrastructure and heat mitigation for dense neighborhoods.

## 6) Governance and Public Benefit Metrics
- Track outcomes: rent-to-income ratios, vacancy levels, permit times, and displacement rates.
- Publish district-level scorecards for affordability, resilience, and maintenance quality.
- Require public engagement windows and clear appeals paths for major land-use changes.

## 7) Implementation Checklist
- [ ] Create a public land and housing data portal with quarterly updates.
- [ ] Adopt zoning that allows missing-middle housing by right.
- [ ] Establish a citywide anti-displacement fund tied to new development fees.
- [ ] Pilot community land trust partnerships for long-term affordability.
- [ ] Integrate climate risk into appraisal and lending standards.

Real estate decisions shape mobility, health, and opportunity for decades. Treating property as civic infrastructure aligns private returns with public resilience.
