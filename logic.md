# Logic

Logic is the craft of linking statements so conclusions follow reliably from assumptions. A sound argument rests on three pillars:

1. **Clear premises.** Every claim should be explicit enough that it can be tested or challenged.
2. **Valid structure.** The steps between premises and conclusions must follow accepted inference rules.
3. **Transparent language.** Precise terms prevent ambiguity from sneaking into the reasoning chain.

## Common patterns

- **Deduction.** From general rules to specific cases (e.g., all cells have membranes; this sample is a cell; therefore it has a membrane).
- **Induction.** From repeated observations to a general rule, with the understanding that future evidence may revise the rule.
- **Abduction.** Inferring the most likely explanation for an observation, then testing that explanation.

## Practical checklist

- Write down assumptions before debating implications.
- Try to disprove your own conclusion; surviving objections strengthen the claim.
- Separate correlation from causation unless an experimental design justifies the link.
- Track uncertainty explicitly, noting where probability rather than certainty applies.

Good logic does not guarantee truth, but it provides the most reliable path for approaching it.
