# Decency

Decency is the everyday practice of treating people with respect even when nothing demands it. It is not flamboyant kindness or strategic politeness; it is a steady baseline of regard that keeps communities livable.

## Traits of decent behavior
- **Consistency under pressure**: Standards do not vanish when deadlines shrink or tempers rise.
- **Attention to impact**: Words and actions are weighed for how they land, not just how they feel to deliver.
- **Quiet accountability**: Mistakes are owned without theatrics, and amends are made without delay.
- **Room for dignity**: Everyone retains the right to decline, question, or need time without being punished for it.

## Ways to build decency
- **Set small defaults**: Keep meetings on time, respond with clarity, and honor shared spaces. These modest habits signal reliability.
- **Separate critique from contempt**: Disagree with ideas while still signaling respect for the person speaking.
- **Notice asymmetries**: Offer extra patience to newcomers, translate jargon, and redistribute context so participation is fair.
- **Protect the absent**: Speak of those not present with the same care you would if they were in the room.

Decency rarely makes headlines, but it shapes whether collaboration feels safe or draining. By keeping respect automatic and repair swift, it creates a floor of trust strong enough to support hard conversations and ambitious work.
