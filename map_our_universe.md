# Map Our Universe

## Executive summary
Mapping our universe means describing **what exists**, **where it is**, and **how it moves** across scales from the solar system to the cosmic web. This guide outlines a practical, scale-by-scale map: the structures we can observe, the techniques we use to chart them, and the data products that tie the map together.

## 1. The nested structure of the universe
A usable map starts with the hierarchy of cosmic structure. Each layer has distinct observables and catalog types.

### Local scale (0–100 AU): the solar system
- **Objects:** Sun, planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud (inferred).
- **Mapping tools:** Radar ranging, spacecraft telemetry, astrometry, occultations.
- **Map products:** Ephemerides and orbital element catalogs.

### Stellar neighborhood (1–1,000 pc)
- **Objects:** Nearby stars, star clusters, local interstellar medium (ISM), dust clouds.
- **Mapping tools:** Parallax (Gaia), spectroscopy for velocities and composition, dust extinction maps.
- **Map products:** 3D star catalogs, ISM density maps, local bubble models.

### Galactic scale (1–100 kpc)
- **Objects:** Milky Way disk, bulge, halo, spiral arms, satellite galaxies.
- **Mapping tools:** HI 21 cm surveys, CO molecular maps, stellar kinematics, variable stars (Cepheids, RR Lyrae).
- **Map products:** Milky Way mass model, rotation curve, spiral arm reconstructions.

### Local Group (0.1–3 Mpc)
- **Objects:** Milky Way, Andromeda, Triangulum, dwarf galaxies, intra-group medium.
- **Mapping tools:** Distance ladders, proper motions, galaxy surveys.
- **Map products:** Local Group membership and dynamics maps.

### Large-scale structure (3 Mpc–10 Gpc)
- **Objects:** Galaxy clusters, superclusters, filaments, voids (the cosmic web).
- **Mapping tools:** Redshift surveys, weak lensing, baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements.
- **Map products:** 3D galaxy distribution, matter density field, filament and void catalogs.

### Cosmic horizon (10–46 Gly)
- **Objects:** Observable universe boundary, cosmic microwave background (CMB), early structure.
- **Mapping tools:** CMB anisotropies, primordial element abundance, large-scale polarization patterns.
- **Map products:** CMB sky maps, inflation constraints, early-universe power spectra.

## 2. What we map: matter, energy, and motion
A complete universe map tracks more than galaxies. It includes hidden components and dynamics.

- **Baryonic matter:** Stars, gas, dust, planets.
- **Dark matter:** Inferred from gravity (rotation curves, lensing).
- **Dark energy:** Inferred from cosmic expansion history.
- **Motion and time:** Proper motions, redshifts, expansion rate, mergers.

## 3. Core techniques (the “toolkit”)

### Distance measurement ladder
- **Parallax → standard candles (Cepheids, SNe Ia) → redshift-distance relations.**

### Spectroscopy
- **Redshift:** Galaxy velocities and distances.
- **Composition:** Elemental abundances, star-formation history.

### Imaging and sky surveys
- **Deep, wide-field mapping** to build catalogs of billions of sources.

### Gravitational lensing
- **Weak lensing** for dark matter maps.
- **Strong lensing** for mass distributions of clusters.

### Time-domain astronomy
- **Transients** (supernovae, GRBs) to map distances and star formation.

## 4. A practical “map” you can build from public data
If you were assembling a multi-scale map today, you could combine:

1. **Solar system ephemerides** (JPL Horizons).
2. **Gaia 3D star catalog** for the local neighborhood.
3. **HI and CO surveys** to map Milky Way gas.
4. **SDSS / DESI / Euclid** for large-scale galaxy distributions.
5. **Planck / WMAP** CMB sky maps for the early universe.
6. **Weak lensing surveys** for dark matter overlays.

## 5. A simple “map view” checklist
For any map layer, ask:
- **What’s being traced?** (stars, gas, dark matter, radiation)
- **How is distance measured?** (parallax, standard candle, redshift)
- **What’s the resolution?** (arcseconds, parsecs, megaparsecs)
- **What’s the uncertainty?** (systematic bias, selection effects)

## Bottom line
Mapping our universe is a **multi-layer, multi-method synthesis**. The map is not a single image but a stack of surveys—each tied together by distance measurements, motion, and gravity—to reveal the universe from our backyard out to the cosmic horizon.
