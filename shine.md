# Shine

Light is more than a beam; it is a practice. When people say "shine," they rarely talk about lumens. They mean showing up without shrinking, knowing that brilliance does not require burning others. 

To shine is to be directional. A lighthouse keeps its beam steady even when waves slam against the rocks. Likewise, a person who shines knows their values and aims, using them as the lens that shapes their light. Without focus, light diffuses into haze. With focus, it can guide ships home.

Shine is also relational. The glow from a campfire warms everyone around it. It does not choose favorites. Personal radiance works the same way: the more generously it is shared, the safer the circle becomes. 

This radiance needs maintenance. Lamps are trimmed, lenses cleaned, batteries recharged. Human energy benefits from the same rituals—rest, reflection, and deliberate pauses to check the direction of the beam.

There is a quiet shine, too. It arrives when someone listens fully, when they stay present through another's uncertainty, when they make room for others to speak. This glow does not seek applause; it simply refuses to leave anyone in the dark.

To shine is to combine steadiness, generosity, and curiosity. It is the courage to remain visible and the humility to illuminate more than yourself.
