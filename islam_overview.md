# Islam Overview

Islam is a monotheistic Abrahamic faith centered on the belief that there is one God (Allah) and that Muhammad is His final prophet. It emphasizes moral responsibility, community welfare, and mindful worship rooted in the Qur'an and the teachings of the Prophet.

## Core Beliefs
- **Tawhid (Oneness of God):** God is singular, without partners or equals, and is both transcendent and intimately aware of creation.
- **Prophethood:** God has sent messengers throughout history, culminating with Prophet Muhammad, whose teachings are preserved in the Qur'an and Sunnah.
- **Revelation:** The Qur'an is considered the final, preserved word of God, providing guidance on faith, ethics, and social life.
- **Angels and the Unseen:** Belief in angels as God’s servants, as well as the Day of Judgment, accountability, and life after death.
- **Divine Decree:** Trust that God’s knowledge and wisdom encompass all events, balanced with human responsibility for choices.

## Five Pillars of Practice
1. **Shahada (Declaration of Faith):** Bearing witness that there is no deity but God and Muhammad is His messenger.
2. **Salah (Prayer):** Performing five daily prayers that connect the believer to God and mark the rhythm of the day.
3. **Zakat (Charitable Almsgiving):** Contributing a portion of wealth to support those in need and purify one’s possessions.
4. **Sawm (Fasting in Ramadan):** Abstaining from food, drink, and harmful speech from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan to cultivate self-discipline and empathy.
5. **Hajj (Pilgrimage to Mecca):** Undertaking the pilgrimage at least once in a lifetime if physically and financially able, emphasizing unity and humility.

## Ethics and Daily Life
- **Compassion and Justice:** Central principles guiding interpersonal conduct, community building, and social equity.
- **Family and Community:** Strong emphasis on family bonds, caring for neighbors, and cooperative community life.
- **Knowledge and Reflection:** Seeking knowledge is encouraged as a form of worship, including learning from both religious and worldly sciences.
- **Moderation:** Avoiding excess and striving for balance in worship, work, and personal habits.

## Diversity Within Islam
- **Major Branches:** Sunni and Shia traditions share core beliefs while differing in certain historical interpretations and practices.
- **Jurisprudential Schools:** Multiple schools of thought contribute to varied legal opinions on worship, ethics, and social matters, reflecting regional and historical contexts.
- **Cultural Expressions:** Muslim communities express faith through diverse languages, art forms, and customs while aligning with overarching principles.

## Spiritual Dimension
- **Remembrance (Dhikr):** Practices that cultivate mindfulness of God, such as reciting prayers and reflecting on divine attributes.
- **Purification of the Heart:** Encouraging sincerity, gratitude, patience, and humility while avoiding arrogance and envy.
- **Hope and Trust:** Balancing reverence for God with optimism in His mercy and guidance.

## Contribution and Coexistence
Islamic teachings encourage collaboration for the common good, respecting human dignity, and honoring the rights of neighbors and guests. Across history, Muslim societies have contributed to science, philosophy, art, and trade, demonstrating that faith and curiosity can coexist and flourish together.
