# Sunbath

The morning lands gently like a towel over shoulders, warm and damp with citrus light.
Street cats stretch in geometry class, their backs describing parabolas across the bricks.
People carry shadows like folded umbrellas, only to forget them on park benches.

By noon the air is a quiet kiln, firing clay cups of thought until glaze appears.
Laundry lines become prayer flags, blessing passerby with cotton ghosts and soap.
Someone counts the clouds—three sailboats, one sleeping whale, one impossible city.

Afternoon tilts the world into sepia; cicadas play a needle on the vinyl leaves.
A child traces the outline of the sun on the sidewalk, chalk squeaking like a violin test.
Neighbors trade peaches over the fence, as if sunlight were a currency needing change.

When dusk unbuttons the heat, the skin remembers the patience of light.
The day cools into a bowl of tea; the wind tastes faintly of mint and salt.
We fall asleep with freckles of memory, a constellation we earned by simply being here.
