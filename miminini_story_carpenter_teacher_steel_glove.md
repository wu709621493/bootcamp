# Miminini: The Carpenter Teacher and His Steel Glove

In the little town of Miminini, a carpenter teacher named Master Lin ran a tiny classroom behind his workshop. Every morning he taught his students how to read, how to measure, and how to make sturdy joints that could hold a roof against rain.

Master Lin had a secret pride: a steel glove he forged himself. It fit his right hand like a second skin, with polished knuckles and a soft leather lining. He used it when shaping stubborn wood, and it never slipped.

One day, he gathered his students and said, “A tool is a promise. This glove is my promise to work safely and with care.” He tapped the steel on the workbench and the sound rang like a small bell.

The children asked if it was heavy. “Heavy enough to remind me to be patient,” he replied. Then he showed them how the glove could protect his fingers when he carved a tight groove.

Word spread through Miminini. Parents visited after school to see the glove, and Master Lin smiled as he explained how he made it. He never pushed it with loud boasts. He simply told stories about the mistakes he learned from, and how the glove helped him keep his hands steady.

By the end of the season, the glove became a symbol in town. People said, “Work like Master Lin: steady, safe, and kind.” The carpenter teacher kept teaching, the steel glove gleaming softly, and the town of Miminini kept building better days.
