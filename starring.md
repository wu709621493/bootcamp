# Starring (Star-Seeing)

To be starring is to be star-seeing: to look up long enough that your posture changes. It is a practice of attention that tilts the day away from errands and back toward wonder. Even a city sky, thin with light and noise, carries a few bright points that keep their appointments.

Star-seeing is not just about distance. It is about patience. A star has already traveled through time to reach your eyes, and the act of looking meets that long journey with a quiet welcome. The reward is not a conclusion but a widening—the sense that the map of your worries is not the whole sky.

Starring also asks for humility. The stars do not perform for us; they simply keep their rhythm. When we watch, we join a chorus of viewers across generations who found steadiness in that rhythm. The gaze upward becomes a reminder that life can be both small and significant at once.

To practice starring is to make room for awe. It is choosing a pause, even a brief one, to stand under the vastness and let it rearrange your scale. The sky does not solve our problems, but it gives them a horizon.
