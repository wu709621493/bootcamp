# Note: Notre-Dame de Paris

Notre-Dame de Paris is a Gothic cathedral on the Île de la Cité in Paris, renowned for its flying buttresses, rose windows, and sculpted portals. Begun in the 12th century and largely completed by the mid-14th century, it became a landmark of medieval craftsmanship, liturgical life, and civic identity. The cathedral’s architectural innovations helped define the High Gothic style, emphasizing verticality, light, and structural daring.

The building has also served as a cultural symbol. It hosted coronations, funerals, and national commemorations, and it was immortalized in Victor Hugo’s 1831 novel *Notre-Dame de Paris*. In April 2019, a major fire damaged the roof and spire, spurring a global restoration effort. Ongoing reconstruction focuses on preserving historic fabric while rebuilding lost timber and lead elements with meticulous care.

For visitors and readers alike, Notre-Dame stands as a layered record of Parisian history: a place where stonework, music, and ritual meet, and where restoration underscores the enduring value of cultural heritage.
