# To Sculpt

To sculpt is to listen to what already exists inside the stone. Each strike of the chisel is less an act of force than an act of translation, moving a hidden contour from thought into daylight.

## Lessons from the studio
- **Patience in removal.** Every subtraction carries risk, so craft unfolds as a sequence of small, deliberate edits rather than a single decisive blow.
- **Dialogue with material.** The stone resists, redirects, and sometimes surprises. Progress comes when the artist responds to these signals instead of overpowering them.
- **Clarity through silhouette.** Shadows reveal shape before details do; seeing the whole form early keeps later refinements aligned with purpose.
- **Embracing dust.** The floor fills with fragments, reminders that refinement demands letting go of what once seemed necessary.

## Carrying the practice elsewhere
Sculpting teaches that focus is an art of omission. Whether designing a program, shaping a policy, or pruning a schedule, refinement is the steady removal of noise until what matters can stand on its own.
