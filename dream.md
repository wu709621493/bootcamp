# Dream

Dreams arrive as soft passengers from the day we leave behind. They ferry questions our waking mind refuses to carry: Who might we become if gravity loosened its hold? What songs would the horizon hum if it could breathe through us?

A dream is not a script. It is a rehearsal for imagination, a messy theater where time folds and colors bend. The stage lights flicker between fear and wonder, and every prop is carved from memory. We move through these scenes barefoot, collecting clues about the lives we have not yet dared to live.

Sometimes a dream is a ladder, each rung made of courage thin as glass. Climbing it means trusting that the fragile will hold. Other times it is a river at night: dark, cold, and carrying a current toward unfamiliar shores. Wading in asks us to surrender to direction without map or compass.

To dream well is to listen. Not for logic, but for resonance—the tone that vibrates when a hidden desire speaks. In that vibration we meet our truest teachers: the child still fearless, the elder patient enough to wait for dawn, the stranger who is simply our own reflection.

Morning does not end the dream. It hands us tools: a pen, a promise, a small resolve to turn the impossible into practice. Each day we are offered this bargain—to trade sleep's generous illusions for deliberate steps. The dream is the blueprint; the waking hours are the worksite.

Carry your dream gently, but build with urgency. The world needs the architectures you sketch in the dark.
