# Catch-22 Meteors

A meteor shower over the desert looked like a simple spectacle until the observatory realized it was stuck in a catch-22. They needed data to justify scrambling telescopes, but they needed telescopes in position to gather that data. The skies kept burning, and the scientists kept debating.

## The paradox

1. **Observation requires authorization.** The agency demands proof of scientific merit before allocating time on the main array.
2. **Proof requires observation.** Every fragment streaks by before peripheral sensors can resolve its spectrum.
3. **Safety complicates both.** The meteor paths skim commercial air corridors, forcing regulators to weigh risk against research.

The loop traps everyone: pilots want guidance, scientists want spectra, and administrators want certainty. No one can act without the others acting first.

## Breaking the loop

- The control room assigns one portable spectrograph to record even partial trails, seeding the missing evidence.
- Regulators accept probabilistic models instead of full tracks, unlocking emergency authorization.
- Once the first spectra confirm metallic inclusions, the main array joins in, and the data cascade breaks the stalemate.

## Lessons

The meteor storm ends with no damage, but the process teaches the team that some problems only resolve when a small, decisive move snaps the catch-22. Even in celestial crises, a minimal viable observation can open the sky.
