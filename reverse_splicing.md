# Reverse Splicing

Reverse splicing is a process where an excised intron RNA inserts itself back into a target RNA molecule.

## Core idea

In normal splicing, introns are removed from pre-mRNA and exons are joined.
In reverse splicing, the intron performs the opposite direction of chemistry:

1. The intron RNA recognizes a sequence in a target RNA.
2. The intron catalyzes insertion into that RNA.
3. The target RNA becomes interrupted by the inserted intron sequence.

## Biological relevance

- Best known in **group II introns**.
- Can contribute to mobility of introns and RNA-based genome evolution pathways.
- Helps support models for how catalytic RNAs may have functioned in early evolution.

## Why it matters

Reverse splicing links RNA catalysis, intron mobility, and the broader idea that RNA can act both as information carrier and enzyme.
