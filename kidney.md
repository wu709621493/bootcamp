# Kidney Health Overview

Kidneys filter blood, regulate electrolytes, balance fluids, and produce hormones like erythropoietin and renin. Each kidney contains about a million nephrons that filter around 180 liters of plasma per day, ultimately producing 1–2 liters of urine.

## Key Functions
- **Filtration and excretion:** Glomeruli filter waste like urea and creatinine while reclaiming needed solutes in the tubules.
- **Fluid and electrolyte balance:** Adjust sodium, potassium, calcium, and phosphate levels via hormone-sensitive transport.
- **Acid-base regulation:** Reabsorb bicarbonate and secrete hydrogen ions to maintain pH.
- **Endocrine roles:** Release renin (blood pressure control), erythropoietin (red blood cell production), and activate vitamin D for calcium metabolism.

## Early Warning Signs of Kidney Problems
- Swelling in legs, ankles, or around the eyes from fluid retention.
- Changes in urination frequency, volume, or appearance (foamy or dark urine).
- Persistent fatigue or shortness of breath due to anemia or fluid buildup.
- Elevated blood pressure that becomes harder to control.
- Nausea, metallic taste, or reduced appetite as toxins accumulate.

## Prevention and Daily Care
- **Hydration:** Maintain steady water intake unless a clinician advises fluid restriction.
- **Blood pressure and glucose control:** Keep hypertension and diabetes managed to protect nephrons.
- **Heart-healthy diet:** Emphasize fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins; limit sodium (<2,300 mg/day), added sugars, and saturated fats.
- **Medication safety:** Use NSAIDs sparingly, avoid double-dosing with over-the-counter pain relievers, and check dose adjustments for existing kidney disease.
- **Avoid smoking and limit alcohol:** Both increase cardiovascular strain and accelerate kidney damage.
- **Regular screening:** Annual eGFR and urine albumin checks for people with diabetes, hypertension, or family history.

## When to Seek Care
Seek prompt medical attention for rapid weight gain with swelling, sudden decline in urine output, severe flank pain, or if urinalysis shows blood or heavy protein. Early consultation with a nephrologist can slow progression and preserve kidney function.
