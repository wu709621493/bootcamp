# Ligand and Go-kart
## A Caltech Romance Play

### Dramatis Personae
- **Lina Grant** — a chemistry graduate student who studies receptor binding.
- **Karthik "Kart" Rao** — a mechanical engineering student rebuilding electric go-karts.
- **Professor Hale** — an exacting advisor with an unexpectedly poetic streak.
- **Maya** — Lina’s labmate and confidante.
- **Jules** — Kart’s teammate on the race club.

---

## Act I: The Binding Site

*Setting: Late afternoon at Caltech. The courtyard between Noyes Laboratory and a student machine shop. A whiteboard leans against a bench, half-covered in reaction mechanisms and half in drivetrain sketches.*

**Lina** *(balancing a box of pipette tips and a notebook)*: If one more person asks whether ligands are "basically molecular hugs," I may dissolve into solvent.

**Kart** *(rolling out from the shop, covered in graphite smudges)*: Depends. Are they selective hugs, reversible hugs, or "irreversibly changed your life" hugs?

**Lina** *(blinks, then laughs despite herself)*: Who are you, and why do you speak fluent physical chemistry?

**Kart**: Karthik. People call me Kart, which makes introductions at the go-kart track deeply confusing.

**Lina**: Lina. People call me "the one who books the NMR at 3 a.m."

**Kart** *(pointing to whiteboard)*: You dropped your notebook. Your equation just escaped into my torque calculations.

**Lina** *(picks it up)*: Sorry. I was mapping binding affinity data and—

**Kart**: And accidentally discovered my gear ratio has the emotional stability of an unstable intermediate?

**Lina**: Exactly that.

*(A brief silence, companionable rather than awkward.)*

**Kart**: I’ve seen you crossing here every evening. Noyes to library, library to Noyes. Same pace every day.

**Lina**: I’ve seen you too. You test motors when everyone else is eating dinner.

**Kart**: Lower campus gets quieter. Easier to hear if something’s wrong.

**Lina**: Same reason I run spectra at night.

*(They regard each other with the startled recognition of people who have been adjacent for months.)*

**Maya** *(offstage, shouting)*: Lina! Your receptor doesn’t care about your feelings, but your cells need feeding!

**Lina** *(calling back)*: Coming!

*(To Kart)*
Tomorrow at five? Bring the gear ratio tragedy. I’ll bring coffee and unsolicited kinetic opinions.

**Kart**: Deal. I’ll trade you unsolicited chassis opinions.

*Blackout.*

---

## Act II: Activation Energy

*Setting: The Caltech go-kart track at dusk. Orange cones, spare batteries, a folding table with tools and two untouched sandwiches. A helmet sits beside a stack of journal articles.*

**Jules** *(tightening a bolt)*: You invited a chemist to fix a kart?

**Kart**: I invited a scientist who notices patterns before they become disasters.

**Jules**: That sounded suspiciously romantic.

**Kart**: It’s interdisciplinary.

*(Lina enters, carrying coffee and a binder.)*

**Lina**: I brought caffeine and a plot that implies your acceleration curve is flirting with catastrophe.

**Jules** *(accepting coffee)*: I support this collaboration.

*(Montage feel: Lina timing lap intervals; Kart adjusting axle alignment; both arguing over data density; both smiling more than either intends.)*

**Lina**: Your motor response spikes here. It’s like a receptor saturated at low concentration—

**Kart**: So I’m overdosing the system with current.

**Lina**: Dramatically.

**Kart**: You say that like it’s a personality diagnosis.

**Lina**: Would I do that?

**Kart**: Constantly.

*(They run a test lap. The kart moves smoother, cleaner.)*

**Jules** *(cheering)*: It’s stable!

**Kart** *(pulling off helmet, breathless)*: That was the best run all semester.

**Lina**: You listened to my weird analogy.

**Kart**: You listened to my weird machine noises.

*(A quiet beat. Campus lights flick on.)*

**Kart**: Lina, do you ever feel like Caltech teaches us to solve everything except timing?

**Lina**: Constantly. I can model binding kinetics but not when to say what I mean.

**Kart**: Then let me be inefficient for once. I like you.

**Lina** *(exhales, almost relieved)*: Good. I like you too.

**Jules** *(backing away theatrically)*: I am suddenly very interested in that faraway toolbox.

*Blackout.*

---

## Act III: Equilibrium

*Setting: Professor Hale’s office. Floor-to-ceiling books, three whiteboards, one model go-kart wheel perched inexplicably beside a crystallography textbook.*

**Professor Hale** *(reading proposal)*: "Coupled analysis of ligand-binding models and electric kart control response."

**Lina**: We think the mathematical framework translates.

**Kart**: And if we can predict nonlinear response from sparse data—

**Professor Hale**: You can make both safer systems and better models. Yes. I know.

*(He looks over his glasses.)*

Are you two prepared for what collaboration does to a schedule and a heart rate?

**Lina**: We already have elevated heart rates.

**Kart**: But now with error bars.

**Professor Hale** *(deadpan)*: Excellent. Quantified feelings.

*(He signs.)*

You have six weeks before symposium. Do not confuse infatuation with reproducibility.

**Lina**: We won’t.

**Kart**: Probably.

*Lights shift to symposium night: poster boards, students in clusters, excited noise. Lina and Kart stand by a shared poster titled “Cross-Domain Response Curves: From Receptors to Racetracks.”*

**Maya** *(reading poster)*: You two turned flirtation into publishable methodology.

**Jules**: And the kart now corners like it has tenure.

**Lina** *(to Kart, softly)*: Remember that first day? Your graphite fingerprints were on my notebook for a week.

**Kart**: You kept them.

**Lina**: They looked like data points.

**Kart**: Want to take one lap after this? No crowds. Just us.

**Lina**: Only if afterward you help me run a final assay.

**Kart**: Deal. Motion and molecules.

*(They clasp hands: not dramatic, just certain.)*

**Professor Hale** *(passing by, without looking up from program)*: Ah. Equilibrium at last.

*Lights fade as distant electric motors hum like a soft overture.*

---

### Epilogue

At Caltech, they would later say the project worked because two disciplines found a common language.

Lina and Kart knew better:

It worked because they learned to listen for signal in each other’s noise.
