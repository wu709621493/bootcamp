# Project Fume

## Purpose
Project Fume reduces hazardous air pollution from industrial, household, and transportation sources by combining rapid monitoring, targeted mitigation, and long-term clean-air infrastructure. The plan prioritizes public health, equitable protections for vulnerable communities, and measurable emissions reductions.

## Guiding principles
1. **Health-first outcomes**: Focus on lowering exposure to harmful particulates, volatile organic compounds, and combustion byproducts.
2. **Community protection**: Prioritize neighborhoods with high pollution burdens and limited access to healthcare.
3. **Data transparency**: Share air-quality data, incident reports, and mitigation progress through public dashboards.
4. **Source accountability**: Pair enforcement with technical assistance so emitters can comply quickly.
5. **Practical transitions**: Promote clean alternatives that are affordable and reliable for households and businesses.

## Focus areas
- **Monitoring and alerts**: Deploy low-cost sensor networks, mobile sampling units, and real-time alerts for high-exposure events.
- **Industrial controls**: Retrofit facilities with scrubbers, leak detection, and maintenance protocols targeting high-emission processes.
- **Household ventilation**: Expand clean-cooking programs, indoor air filters, and ventilation upgrades for dense housing.
- **Transportation emissions**: Accelerate fleet electrification, anti-idling enforcement, and clean freight corridors near schools and clinics.
- **Workplace safety**: Strengthen occupational exposure limits and provide protective equipment for high-risk jobs.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Map exposure hotspots using historical emissions data, health records, and community reporting channels.
2. Install sensor networks and publish real-time readings with clear health guidance during spikes.
3. Launch a retrofit and inspection sprint for top emitters with compliance milestones and financing support.
4. Distribute household mitigation kits and clean-cooking options through local clinics and community hubs.
5. Track reductions quarterly, adjust enforcement priorities, and scale solutions across additional districts.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Data gaps**: Cross-validate sensor data with lab-grade sampling and third-party audits.
- **Industrial resistance**: Offer staged compliance timelines, grants, and public recognition for early adopters.
- **Household affordability**: Provide subsidies, bulk procurement, and microfinancing for clean appliances.
- **Enforcement capacity**: Train inspectors, streamline reporting, and use automated alerts for violations.
- **Public mistrust**: Build advisory councils and publish plain-language progress updates.

## Success metrics
- Reduced average PM2.5 and VOC concentrations in identified hotspots.
- Percentage of high-emitting facilities meeting upgraded control standards.
- Number of households adopting clean-cooking or filtration solutions.
- Lower respiratory-related emergency visits in prioritized neighborhoods.
- Sustained public engagement through reporting and survey feedback.
