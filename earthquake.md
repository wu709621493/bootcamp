# Earthquake Readiness Guide

## Why Earthquakes Matter
Earthquakes release accumulated tectonic stress as sudden ground motion. Even moderate events can disrupt power, water, transport, and health services, so preparation focuses on reducing physical hazards and speeding recovery.

## Prepare Before Shaking Starts
- **Secure your space**: Bolt bookshelves, water heaters, and gas appliances; use latches on cabinet doors; store heavy items low.
- **Plan communication**: Agree on an out-of-area contact and designate meeting points in case local networks fail.
- **Assemble supplies**: Maintain two weeks of water (4 liters per person per day), nonperishable food, flashlights, batteries, a radio, medications, cash, and copies of key documents.
- **Check utilities**: Know how to shut off gas, water, and electricity; install automatic gas shutoff valves where permitted.
- **Review insurance**: Standard homeowners policies typically exclude earthquake damage—purchase coverage if risk warrants it.
- **Strengthen structures**: Retrofit cripple walls, anchor sill plates to foundations, brace masonry chimneys, and install seismic straps on heavy fixtures.

## What to Do During an Earthquake
- **Indoors**: Drop, cover, and hold on under sturdy furniture. Stay away from windows and exterior walls. Do not run outside during strong shaking.
- **Outdoors**: Move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and utility wires. Drop to the ground until shaking stops.
- **Driving**: Pull over safely, set the parking brake, and avoid stopping on bridges, overpasses, or near power lines.

## After the Shaking
- **Check for injuries and hazards**: Apply first aid, look for gas leaks (smell or hissing), damaged wiring, and structural issues before re-entering buildings.
- **Expect aftershocks**: Be ready to drop, cover, and hold on again. Avoid damaged structures until inspected.
- **Use phones sparingly**: Preserve bandwidth for emergency calls; rely on texts or radio updates when possible.
- **Document damage**: Photograph impacts for insurance claims and report utility issues to providers.

## Community and Infrastructure Resilience
- **Microzonation awareness**: Understand local soil conditions and liquefaction or landslide susceptibility; follow zoning and retrofit guidance tailored to your area.
- **Critical lifelines**: Encourage flexible pipeline joints at fault crossings, seismic shutoff valves, and backup power for hospitals, data centers, and water systems.
- **Mutual aid networks**: Coordinate with neighbors for shared tools, childcare, and welfare checks on vulnerable residents.

## Quick Checklist
- Home secured and heavy items anchored.
- Family communication and reunification plan set.
- Two-week emergency kit stocked and refreshed every six months.
- Utility shutoff tools accessible; gas shutoff valve identified.
- Insurance coverage and important documents reviewed and backed up.
