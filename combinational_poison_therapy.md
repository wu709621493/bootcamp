# Combinational Poison Therapy

Combinational poison therapy is a treatment strategy that uses **multiple toxic agents together** to achieve a stronger therapeutic effect than any single agent alone.

## Core idea

The approach relies on combining compounds that attack different cellular pathways so that target cells (for example, cancer cells or pathogens) are less able to adapt.

## Why combinations are used

- **Synergy:** two agents together can produce more effect than the sum of each alone.
- **Resistance reduction:** multi-pathway pressure can make resistance less likely.
- **Dose balancing:** each component may be used at a lower dose to reduce single-drug toxicity.

## Major risks

- Overlapping side effects can increase harm to healthy tissue.
- Drug–drug interactions can alter metabolism and unexpectedly raise toxicity.
- Narrow therapeutic windows demand careful monitoring and patient-specific adjustment.

## Practical safeguards

- Start from mechanistic rationale and preclinical interaction data.
- Use stepwise dosing and frequent toxicity checks.
- Define stop rules and rescue protocols before treatment begins.

In short, combinational poison therapy is potentially powerful but must be designed with strict safety controls and evidence-driven dose management.
