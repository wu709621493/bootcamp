# Nihon (Japan)

Nihon, or Nippon, is the Japanese name for Japan. It is an island nation in East Asia known for a long continuity of culture and rapid periods of modernization.

## Quick facts
- **Capital:** Tokyo
- **Main islands:** Honshu, Hokkaido, Kyushu, Shikoku, and Okinawa (Ryukyu archipelago)
- **Population:** Over 120 million people
- **Government:** Constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary democracy
- **Currency:** Japanese yen (JPY)

## Cultural highlights
- **Language and writing:** Modern Japanese uses a blend of kanji (characters of Chinese origin) and two syllabaries, hiragana and katakana, collectively called kana. Romaji transcribes Japanese sounds using the Latin alphabet for learners and signage.
- **Cuisine:** Washoku, traditional Japanese cuisine, emphasizes seasonal ingredients and balance. Sushi, sashimi, ramen, tempura, and okonomiyaki are globally known dishes. Regional specialties—such as Hokkaido seafood, Kansai okonomiyaki and takoyaki, and Kyushu tonkotsu ramen—show local diversity.
- **Arts:** Classical forms include Noh and Kabuki theater, the tea ceremony (chanoyu), and ikebana flower arrangement. Contemporary culture spans film, anime, manga, design, and video games.
- **Festivals:** Shinto and Buddhist calendars anchor yearly events such as New Year (Shōgatsu), cherry blossom viewing (hanami), summer matsuri with fireworks (hanabi taikai), and autumn harvest celebrations.
- **Aesthetics:** Principles like wabi-sabi (beauty in impermanence), ma (space and timing), and mottainai (avoidance of waste) influence design and daily life.

## Historical sketch
- **Ancient and classical periods:** Early Yamato state formation (Kofun and Asuka periods) led to the adoption of Buddhism and Chinese-inspired governance. The Heian period fostered court literature such as *The Tale of Genji*.
- **Feudal era:** From the Kamakura to Edo periods, shogunates and samurai dominated politics. A policy of sakoku limited foreign trade during the Edo era while urban culture thrived in cities like Edo (Tokyo), Osaka, and Kyoto.
- **Modern era:** The Meiji Restoration (1868) industrialized Japan and centralized power. Post–World War II reconstruction transformed the nation into a leading economy focused on technology, manufacturing, and design.

## Geography and environment
Japan spans a volcanic archipelago along the Pacific Ring of Fire. Tectonic activity produces onsen (hot springs) but also earthquakes and occasional volcanic eruptions. The climate ranges from subarctic Hokkaido to subtropical Okinawa, creating distinct regional ecosystems. Forests cover much of the country, and conservation coexists with dense coastal megacities.

## Society and innovation
- **Education and research:** High literacy and strong STEM education support leadership in robotics, materials science, and transportation. Japan pioneered high-speed rail with the Shinkansen in 1964 and continues to expand maglev projects.
- **Urban life:** Efficient public transit and walkable neighborhoods characterize major cities. Compact housing and mixed-use development shape daily routines.
- **Sustainability shifts:** Initiatives include energy diversification after the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, circular-economy policies, and ecological restoration of rivers and coastal wetlands.

## Visiting Japan
Travelers can navigate easily with trains and local IC cards (like Suica and Pasmo). Respectful practices include punctuality, quiet phone use on transit, removing shoes in homes and some inns, and cash or contactless card readiness. Seasonal travel offers contrasting experiences: cherry blossoms in spring, alpine hiking in summer, vibrant foliage in autumn, and snow sports in winter.
