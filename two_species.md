# Two Species Treaty

- **Mutual sightlines**: Each species maps how the other reads the world—scents, colors, electrical pulses—and designs shared spaces where both sets of senses receive clear signals instead of noise.
- **Metabolic exchanges**: Food, waste, and energy loops are planned as complements: one species' exhale, molt, or leaf fall becomes feedstock for the other's growth rather than a pollutant to isolate.
- **Calendar of tolerances**: Seasonal thresholds for heat, salinity, and crowding are published as a public calendar, guiding migration corridors, harvesting windows, and quiet periods before nesting or spawning.
- **Language scaffolds**: Shared vocabularies evolve through patterned sounds, pigment flashes, or gesture sequences tied to immediate actions (approach, retreat, share, wait), reducing the risk of defensive misreads.
- **Conflict triage**: When competition peaks, a jointly trusted arbitration protocol prioritizes slowing the escalation—cool-down territories, resource rationing, and third-party mediation from keystone species or neutral machines.
- **Health beacons**: Biosensors or sentinel individuals broadcast early stress signals (pH shifts, cortisol spikes, erratic schooling) so intervention happens before cascading die-offs or panic flights.
- **Cultural cross-pollination**: Each species hosts observatories where the other can safely watch rituals like mating dances or den-building, embedding respect through repeated exposure rather than surprise encounters.
- **Inheritance rules**: Offspring carry dual registries—parental lineage plus an ecological covenant that enumerates non-negotiables (no toxin release near nurseries, no noise during lunar peaks).
- **Adaptive clauses**: Every decade, the treaty rewrites itself with new data on climate drift, gene flow, and behavior shifts, ensuring co-evolution stays negotiated instead of assumed.
