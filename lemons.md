# Lemons: Bright Acid and Bitter Aroma

Lemons carry a layered flavor profile: sharp citric acid, perfumed oils in the zest, and subtle bitterness in the pith. They lift savory dishes, anchor desserts, and preserve foods when sugar or salt alone is not enough.

## Buying and Storing
- Look for **heavy fruit** with thin, glossy skin; weight signals high juice content.
- Avoid soft spots or dull rind, which hint at drying or internal mold.
- Store at room temperature for a week, or refrigerate in a breathable bag for up to a month.

## Essential Uses
- **Brighten**: Finish grilled vegetables or fried foods with a squeeze to cut fat.
- **Balance**: Add a teaspoon of juice to overly sweet sauces; acidity restores clarity.
- **Preserve**: Salt-packed lemon slices or wedges ferment into mellow, floral flavor bombs for tagines and grain salads.
- **Bake**: Zest carries most of the aromatic oils; grate only the colored skin to avoid bitterness.

## Quick Syrup
1. Zest 2 lemons into 1 cup of sugar; massage until the sugar smells floral.
2. Juice the lemons, then warm the juice and sugar together just until dissolved.
3. Chill and strain; use in iced tea, cocktails, or over yogurt.

## Safety Notes
- Acid can corrode reactive metals. Use glass, stainless steel, or enamel when storing lemon-based mixtures.
- Wash and dry lemons before zesting to avoid waxes or dust in the peel.
