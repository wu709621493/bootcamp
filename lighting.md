# Lighting Design Principles

Lighting shapes the way people perceive a space, influences well-being, and guides attention. Thoughtful design balances aesthetics, safety, and efficiency.

## Goals of Effective Lighting
- Provide visual comfort with controlled glare and balanced luminance.
- Support tasks with appropriate brightness, contrast, and color rendering.
- Enhance mood and storytelling through layered scenes and focal points.
- Conserve energy while maintaining quality of light and minimizing light pollution.

## Layers of Light
1. **Ambient:** Establishes overall illumination, typically through ceiling fixtures, cove lighting, or indirect bounce sources.
2. **Task:** Focused illumination for work surfaces, reading areas, kitchen counters, and desks.
3. **Accent:** Adds drama and hierarchy by highlighting art, textures, architectural details, or landscape elements.
4. **Decorative:** Fixtures that contribute character and visual interest, such as pendants or sculptural luminaires.

## Key Design Considerations
- **Color Temperature:** Warm light (2700–3000K) fosters relaxation; neutral (3500–4000K) supports focus; cooler (5000K) favors alertness and daylight matching.
- **Color Rendering Index (CRI):** CRI 90+ preserves accurate hues in art, food, and skin tones.
- **Glare Control:** Use shielding, louvers, and indirect strategies to reduce discomfort and veiling reflections.
- **Uniformity vs. Contrast:** Balance even coverage for safety with selective contrast for depth and interest.
- **Dimming and Scenes:** Layered controls allow adaptive lighting for changing tasks, circadian support, and energy savings.
- **Daylight Integration:** Pair shading and sensors with daylight harvesting to reduce energy use and maintain visual comfort.
- **Sustainability:** Favor long-life, serviceable luminaires; minimize uplight and spill to protect night skies and wildlife.

## Metrics and Planning
- Perform photometric calculations for illuminance (lux or footcandles) tailored to tasks.
- Set target uniformity ratios (e.g., 3:1 max-to-min) for circulation and safety areas.
- Review luminance of surfaces, not just illuminance, to avoid overly bright or dark planes.
- Specify controls for occupancy, vacancy, and daylight-responsive dimming zones.

## Application Notes
- **Residential:** Layer ambient with localized task lighting; use warm temperatures for living spaces and warmer dimming in bedrooms.
- **Workplace:** Prioritize glare-free task lighting, neutral color temperatures, and flexible control zones for changing layouts.
- **Hospitality:** Emphasize accent and decorative layers to craft mood; conceal sources to keep sightlines clean.
- **Exterior:** Apply cutoff optics, timers, and adaptive controls; light only where needed to maintain safety and limit pollution.

## Maintenance and Longevity
- Document fixture locations, lamp types, drivers, and control systems for future service.
- Plan access for cleaning lenses, replacing drivers, and aiming adjustable fixtures.
- Monitor color shift and lumen depreciation; schedule relamping or module replacement to maintain consistency.

Thoughtful lighting design blends science, craft, and empathy—bringing spaces to life while respecting the people and ecosystems they serve.
