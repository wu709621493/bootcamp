# Metabolism Overview

Metabolism encompasses the network of biochemical reactions that convert nutrients into energy and building blocks while removing waste products. It balances catabolic pathways that break down molecules to release energy with anabolic pathways that synthesize complex molecules required for growth, repair, and maintenance.

## Energy Flow and Core Pathways
- **ATP as energy currency:** Cells capture energy from nutrient oxidation and store it transiently in ATP, coupling ATP hydrolysis to energy-requiring processes such as biosynthesis, transport, and motility.
- **Central carbon metabolism:** Glycolysis, the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation link carbohydrate, fat, and protein catabolism to ATP production via NADH and FADH₂ electron carriers.
- **Fuel flexibility:** Cells adjust fuel selection based on nutrient availability and hormonal state, shifting between glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids; the liver supports systemic needs through glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, and ketogenesis.

## Anabolism and Biosynthesis
- **Macromolecule assembly:** Anabolic pathways use ATP and reducing equivalents (e.g., NADPH) to build carbohydrates (glycogen), lipids (fatty acids, cholesterol), proteins, and nucleic acids.
- **Precursor provision:** TCA cycle intermediates and pentose phosphate pathway products supply carbon skeletons and ribose-5-phosphate for amino acid and nucleotide synthesis.
- **Reductive power:** NADPH from the pentose phosphate pathway and malic enzyme fuels fatty acid synthesis, antioxidant defense (glutathione reduction), and detoxification reactions.

## Regulation and Integration
- **Hormonal control:** Insulin promotes anabolic storage and glucose uptake; glucagon and epinephrine trigger catabolic mobilization (glycogen breakdown, lipolysis, gluconeogenesis) during fasting or stress.
- **Allosteric and covalent modulation:** Key enzymes (e.g., phosphofructokinase-1, pyruvate dehydrogenase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase) respond to metabolite signals (ATP/AMP, citrate, acetyl-CoA) and reversible phosphorylation by kinases and phosphatases.
- **Compartmentalization:** Mitochondria host oxidative catabolism and β-oxidation, cytosol supports glycolysis and fatty acid synthesis, and the endoplasmic reticulum handles lipid elongation and detoxification.

## Metabolic States and Adaptation
- **Fed vs. fasting:** In the fed state, insulin directs glucose storage and lipid synthesis; during fasting, glucagon shifts metabolism to hepatic glucose output, fatty acid oxidation, and ketone body production for brain and muscle use.
- **Exercise response:** Muscle increases glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation, with AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) enhancing glucose uptake and fatty acid oxidation to match energy demand.
- **Stress and inflammation:** Cortisol and cytokines reroute substrates toward gluconeogenesis and acute-phase protein synthesis while suppressing reproductive and growth pathways.

## Clinical and Public Health Relevance
- **Metabolic disorders:** Dysregulation contributes to obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, inborn errors of metabolism, and mitochondrial disorders.
- **Therapeutic targets:** Lifestyle interventions, medications (e.g., metformin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors), and dietary strategies (low-glycemic diets, ketogenic diets) modulate metabolic pathways to restore energy balance.
- **Biomarkers and monitoring:** Blood glucose, lipids, ketones, lactate, and hemoglobin A1c track metabolic status; metabolic profiling and imaging increasingly support precision nutrition and medicine.
